# What is a Market
---
**Market**
> A place (online or offline) where <span style='color:#f7b731'>economic transaction</span> happens between 2 parties

The transactions carried out in the market may involve goods, services, information, currency, or any combination of these between parties.

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Anything involving any transaction is considered a market</span>, even illicit markets where illegal goods are bring transacted.

The government regulates (legal) markets by implementing terms and conditions (e.g., Tax), these are called <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>regulations</span>.

# Market Power
---
**Market power (market dominance)**
> The ability of a firm or group of firms within a market to profitably <span style='color:#f7b731'>charge prices above the competitive level</span> for a sustained period of time.

With <span style='color:#f7b731'>strong market power</span> comes with the ability to <span style='color:#f7b731'>shut out competitors</span>, <span style='color:#f7b731'>charge high prices</span>, and even get away with producing <span style='color:#f7b731'>low quality products.</span>

Economists and courts often use “market power,” “monopoly power,” and “market dominance” interchangeably.

Due to anti consumer and anti competitive issues, huge market power always reduces the economic wealth of society in many ways.

- Sherman Antitrust Act (US)
- M&A regulation
- Competition regulation

# Defining the Relevant Market
---
**Relevant Market**
> A set of products or services that are <span style='color:#f7b731'>considered substitutes by consumers</span>, both in terms of their characteristics and the geographic area where they are offered.

Knowing which market a product is in plays a critical role.
1) **Government**
	Government regulations such as taxes and competition regulations
2) **Businesses** 
	Know their competitors and will set their prices based on substitutions in the same market. At the same time create more innovative products
3) **Customers**
	Enjoy incentives from innovative products, increased choices and competitive prices

## How to Define the Relevant Market

**Government Guidance**
- Singapore Standard Industrial Classification a nation standard for classifying economic activates 
- NAIC Code (US Bureau of Census)

**Intuitive Conditions**
- Similar Product Performance Attributes
- Similar Usage Occasions
- Geographic Markets

**SSNIP**

Also known as <span style='color:#f7b731'>Small but significant non-transitory increase in price</span>. It is use to determine which products are grouped under which market

The core behind this is when a product is increased by a certain amount, and customers do not switch to the next best substitutes when that product(s) is in its own market.

The government specifies the price increase to be around <span style='color:#f7b731'>5 - 10%</span>. This is to <span style='color:#f7b731'>prevent</span> raising the <span style='color:#f7b731'>prices too high</span> which will result in no transaction between customers.

# Market Structures
---
## Perfect Competition

A perfect competition is what the <span style='color:#f7b731'>market strives to becomes but it can never happen</span>.

**Characteristics**
- Many buyers and sellers with small sizes
- Homogeneous Products (Little Differentiation)
- Perfect information for buyers and sellers
- Free market entry and exit
- Equal access to technologies

In the <span style='color:#f7b731'>long run</span>, all firms in the perfect competition will only <span style='color:#f7b731'>generate normal profits</span>.
- If a certain company starts making a profit, other companies will enter and sell the same product (no entry barrier). As a result, supply increases, leading to a decrease in price and, subsequently, a decrease in profits.
- Conversely, companies facing losses will exit the market, reducing the supply and causing the price to rise. Through this process, in the long run, the profits of each company converge to match their average costs
- Due to these characteristics, sellers or providers cannot influence market prices. Both the company and the consumer must accept the market price as given

**Examples**
- Agricultural Market for raw produce, products have little differentiation.

## Monopoly

A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>monopoly</span> is where a company has a <span style='color:#f7b731'>significant power and impact over the market</span> for a particular good or service. This is called <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>monopolistic power</span>. 

**Characteristics**
- Requires huge capital
- No Substitutes
- Technological superiority and control of the resources
- Prices are fixed and can be manipulated
- Declining Product quality as there is no substitutes
- Loss of Innovation as there are very little or no competitors there is no need to be innovated to stay ahead of the curve.

Not all monopoly's are bad, for instance utilities industry are mostly a monopoly. The government mandates it and also regulates it.
### Creative Monopoly

It is a term used which means that a company product or service is <span style='color:#f7b731'>too good that no other firm can compete</span> (serve as a substitute). Which might also give customers more choices.

**Advantages of monopoly**
- Economies of Scale: Reduced average cost due to large scale production
- Stability of prices: Less price fluctuation, making it more price stable
- R&D spending: Due to stable profits, these monopolistic firms can invest more into research and development.

### Is It Easy to Become A Monopoly In a Digital Economy

It is <span style='color:#f7b731'>difficult</span> to become a Monopoly in a digital economy, this is because of;

- High switching cost (not because of price but because of private data and personalized features)
- “Winner takes all” phenomenon
- Economies of scale: High initial fixed cost and low marginal cost of production
- Convergence of digital services: Convergence is the blending of previously separate technologies, processes, and data to create new combinations of products, services, and experiences that reshape industry structures.
- AI & Data analytics: Enable highly differentiated and superior quality products
- Hard to regulate: Unclear market definition

## Oligopoly

An <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>oligopoly</span> consists of a <span style='color:#f7b731'>few companies</span> that combined exert significant influence over a market or sector.

In this case, the firms compete with one another to <span style='color:#20bf6b'>collaborate</span>
- By collaborating, firms can maintain stable prices and avoid destructive price wars
- To prevent the monopoly of a single company

**Advantages of oligopoly**
- Companies often engage in non price competition, such as advertising, product differentiation, and branding
- Oligopolies might engage in joint research and development, which can lead to faster innovations
- Like monopolies, oligopolistic firms can achieve economies of scale, leading to lower consumer prices

# Potter's Five Forces Model
---
- Provides a general view of the firm, its competitors, and the firm’s environment
- Derive five forces that determine the competitive intensity and therefore attractiveness of a market
- A useful tool to understand the attractiveness of an industry and to systematically analyze the external environment surrounding a firm
- The overall industry attractiveness does not imply that every firm in the industry will return the same profitability. Competitive advantage of a firm is achieved by enhancing a firm’s ability to combat the 5 forces

## Entry

The <span style='color:#f7b731'>less time and money</span> it costs for a competitor to enter a company's market and be an effective competitor, the more an <span style='color:#f7b731'>established company's position could be significantly weakened</span>.

An industry with <span style='color:#f7b731'>strong barriers to entry is ideal for existing companies</span> within that industry since the company would be able to charge higher prices and negotiate better terms.

- Entry Cost
- Speed of Adjustment
- Sunk Cost (Startup Cost)
- Economies of Scale
- Network Effects
- Reptation
- Switching Costs
- Government Restrains

**Thread of Entrants is high when**
1) There are very little barriers of entry
2) It requires a lot of financial resources to start a business
## Power of Buyers

The ability that customers have to drive prices lower. It is <span style='color:#f7b731'>affected by how many buyers or customers a company has</span>, how significant each customer is, and how much it would cost a company to find new customers or markets for its output.

A <span style='color:#f7b731'>smaller and more powerful client base means that each customer has more power</span> to negotiate for lower prices and better deals. A company that has many, smaller, independent customers will have an easier time charging higher prices to increase profitability.

- Buyer Concentration
- Price/Value of Substitute Products or Services
- Relationship-Specific Investments
- Customer switching Costs
- Government Restraints

**Buyer's Bargaining Power is high when**
1) It has a large concentrated buying power than enables it to gain volume discounts and / or special terms and services
2) Strong potential for bringing additional integration of services / products
## Power of Supplies

It is <span style='color:#f7b731'>affected by the number of suppliers</span> of key inputs of a good or service, <span style='color:#f7b731'>how unique these inputs are</span>, and how much it would <span style='color:#f7b731'>cost a company to switch to another supplier</span>. The fewer suppliers to an industry, the more a company would depend on a supplier.

As a result, the supplier has more power and can drive up input costs and push for other advantages in trade. On the other hand, when there are many suppliers or low switching costs between rival suppliers, a company can keep its input costs lower and enhance its profits.

- Supplier Concentration
- Price/Value of Alternative inputs
- Relationship-Specific Investments
- Supplier switching Costs
- Government Restraints

**Supplier Power is high when**
1) Domination of supply by a few companies
2) High built up costs
3) Provides benefits through geographic proximity to customers
4) Pose a threat to forward integration into its customer's business

## Industry Rivalry

The larger the number of competitors, along with the number of equivalent products and services they offer, the lesser the power of a company. With more competitors there is a possibility of a undercut. 

Suppliers and buyers seek out a company's competition if they are able to offer a better deal or lower prices. Conversely, when <span style='color:#f7b731'>competitive rivalry is low</span>, a company has <span style='color:#f7b731'>greater power to charge higher prices and set the terms of deals</span> to achieve higher sales and profits.

- Concentration
- Price, Quality or Quality Competition
- Degree of Differentiation
- Switching Cost 
- Timing of Decisions
- Information
- Government Restrains

**Rivalry is intense when**
1) competitions are numerous or are roughly equal in size and power
2) Industry growth is slow precipitating fights for market share
3) Product or service lacks differentiation or switching costs.
## Substitutes and Compliment

Substitute goods or services that can be used in place of a company's products or services pose a threat. When there are <span style='color:#f7b731'>no close substitutes companies will have more power</span> to increase prices and lock in favorable terms. When close substitutes are available, customers will have the option to forgo buying a company's product, and a company's power can be weakened.

- Price / Value of Substitute Products or Services
- Price / Value of complementary products or services
- Network Effects
- Government Restrains

**The threat of substitute is high when**
1) Products or services from other companies satisfy the same customer needs
2) Relative price performance of substitutes is high
3) Switching cost to new products is low
