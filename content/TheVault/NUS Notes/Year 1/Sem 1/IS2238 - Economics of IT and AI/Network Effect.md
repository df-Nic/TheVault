# Externalities
---
It is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>indirect cost or benefit to an uninvolved third party</span> that arises as an effect of another party's activity.

It is basically an event that occurs as a byproduct of another event occurring, this can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>either positive or negative</span>. It focusing on the impact an economic or social activity has on a social welfare.

**Examples:**
- Pollution caused by commuting to work or a chemical spill caused by improperly stored waste are examples of <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>negative externalities</span>.
- A person receiving an education contributes more to society, leading to benefits for the entire community. This is a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>positive externality</span>.
	1) Higher levels of education lower crime rates
	2) Higher education in the STEM field can lead to innovations which benefit from society
	3) Education in arts can contribute to the arts scene

# Network Effect
---
It is a phenomenon where the value or utility a user derives from a goods or service depends on the <span style='color:#f7b731'>number of users</span> in general and user connection in particular, in the product/service network.

To sum it up, when <span style='color:#f7b731'>more people use a particular good or service it improves the value</span> of that good and service. It is usually a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>positive externality</span>.

This happens because, lets say a particular product is used by a person, the likelihood of people close to him to use the same product will increase. As a result, the overall number of users on the <span style='color:#f7b731'>platform grows, leading to a form of positive externality where each user receives an additional value</span>. This increases demand and companies will want to increase prices.

**Congestion**
> It is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>negative network effect</span> whereby too many users can slow a network down, reducing its utility and frustrating network members.

Network effects have become a defining property of the digital economy, since the business model of many digital services is based on <span style='color:#f7b731'>matchmaking</span> (e.g., Uber, Airbnb, Gig economy platforms) and <span style='color:#f7b731'>mediation</span> between users (e.g., YouTube, Facebook)

## Break free from the vicious cycle
- Take Groupon as an example; while it pioneered in the realm of social commerce (daily deals), it faced challenges due to its singular focus on expanding its buyer base.
- Many service providers have had experiences they wouldn't want to repeat.
- Similarly, eBay's growth has been hindered by a decline in buyer experience, which resulted from an influx of too many sellers.
## Network Categorization

**One Sided**
> It is a network in which connections are made within <span style='color:#f7b731'>one group of homogeneous users</span>. There is no specific group and is usually connection between users like what Facebook does

**Two Sided**
>It is a network in which connections are made between <span style='color:#f7b731'>two groups of users that need each other.</span> For example between buyers and sellers. <span style='color:#f7b731'>One sided network can exist in a two sided network</span>.

As more users are in the network, there will be more connections possible, $$\text{Number of Links} = n(n-1)/2$$
The value of a <span style='color:#f7b731'>network increases proportionally to the square of the number of users</span>. According to Metcalfe, every node is of equal benefit.

This does not represent all networks as <span style='color:#f7b731'>not all nodes create connections</span>, For instance in social networks, new entrants that join later and have different interests and expertise may not create connections with existing users. This decrease benefit of each additional user, making the overall network less efficient if costs per users are fixed.

Companies and celebs may have a better node value compared to others.

The value of the network is calculated by the <span style='color:#f7b731'>nodes connected or meaningful links, which is the number of users</span>.

Network effect causes an increase in customer base and thus companies can increase price or change from a free model to a freemium or subscription model

Currently social media companies are finding ways to <span style='color:#f7b731'>penetrate new communication habits of users</span>, go build more meaningful links, instead of attracted more users who might be hard to connect in the network.

**Example:**
The rich content is what enabled YouTube to provide more value to new users rapidly because they are able
to demonstrate immediate value to users through its existing network.
- Users experience benefits when they align the behavior with others in this case users can uploaded content to imitate their favorite content creators
- The platform provides statistical numbers to evaluate the content

A network can be disrupted by making the <span style='color:#f7b731'>incumbent’s network redundant</span>.

**For example**:
- Zoom is actually built on allowing “frictionless” communication between users the service is not meant to
only work for those in its network.
- A Zoom user can start a video call with someone by just sending them a zoom link, and the recipient does not
even need to have a Zoom account.
- This makes it especially useful for sales calls (or any meetings with external stakeholders), removing the need to
exchange personal account information (which is the case with Google Hangouts)
- However, a byproduct of the fact that Zoom has no network effects is that it is in principle easily replaced by other video conferencing services.
- If Zoom servers are down one day, there are many other ways that two people can video conference

# Risk & Information Asymmetry
---
## Risk

**Risk**
> Possibility of loss  / injury

Taking risk is part and parcel of a day to day life, companies will need to take risks in order to profit or move forward.

Factors which determine the risk appetite;
1) Estimates of expected outcome
2) Attitude towards risk

Most individuals are <span style='color:#f7b731'>risk-averse generally</span>.

When a <span style='color:#f7b731'>product quality is uncertain</span>;
1) When uncertainty is high the willingness to pay is lower than the products actual quality
2) No / few high quality goods to be sold in the market which will lead to a potential market failure.

To reduce risk and uncertainty;
1) Informative advertising
2) Free trials / samples
3) Money-back guarantee or return policies
4) Third party quality/reputation certification (e.g., Amazon)
5) Third party quality/reputation review (e.g. ISO)

Free trials works as it serves as a function to;
1) Provide quality signals
2) Overcoming quality uncertainty

However, with free trials, though it is a form of advertising, <span style='color:#f7b731'>there is a sunk cost but the revenue is recovered from future sales</span>.
# Information Asymmetric
---
**Information Asymmetry**
>When one party in a transaction is in possession of more information than the other. In certain transactions, sellers can take advantage of buyers because asymmetric information exists whereby the seller has more knowledge of the good being sold than the buyer.

Digital goods are mostly <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>experience goods</span>
> Goods that can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>accurately evaluated only after the product has been purchased and experienced</span>. Quality is learnt only from experience, there is no way to convince customers of quality and value.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Search goods</span> are goods whose quality may be learned without using or consuming them such as fresh food, appliances and fashion apparel.

Companies want to do second degree price discrimination is to hide information and reduce asymmetric information.
## Solutions to Asymmetric Information

Quality uncertainty may be alleviated by;
- Repeat purchases with consumer learning
- Seller or buyer reputation build up
- Price as a signal for product quality

**Signaling**
>Action taken by the informed party to reveal private information or indicators of hidden characteristics to the uninformed party

Signals must not be easily mimicked by others
	- More costly (harder) to send a false signal
	- Less costly to send a true signal

Examples: education as signal of work ability, eBay reputation as signal of trading integrity

**Screening**
> Action taken by the uninformed party to reveal private information

Attempt by the uninformed party to sort individuals according to their characteristics this is often accomplished through a self selection device. However companies will not want to expose their internal information and internal events

Investors will collect all kinds of information to maximize returns and minimize risks.

Examples: health exam proves health status, second degree price discrimination.
