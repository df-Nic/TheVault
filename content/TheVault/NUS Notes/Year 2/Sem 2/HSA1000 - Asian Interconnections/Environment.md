## Case Study: India

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>green revolution</span>, is the beginning  in the food grain production after consecutive droughts and bad monsoons

The green revolution package included **high-yielding seed varieties**, chemical fertilisers and pesticides, enhanced irrigation technologies. This <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>resulted in higher yields by about 30%</span>.

> [!fail] Unequal accessibility
> It favoured people who **stays near water sources**, rich and poor farmers.

However the government only <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>focuses on yield and economic gain</span> rather than l<span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ong-term sustainability and conserving grownwater</span>.

> [!summary] Soybean popularity
> Farmers prefer soybean due to its high yields and gains and it can be replanted very fast.

There are also **insurance schemes to manage these risk** which bring about the new scheme, but these are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>not available to all</span>.

> [!question] Why not take up the scheme
> But these schemes are 100% subsidised, however it **requires more equipment** cause a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>25-35% additional spending required of farmer</span>

In the end thought these schemes were to help farmers but due to financial inequality, many of the poor farmers were not able to benefit from it.
## Air Pollution in China

In the past China has a very bad air pollution, but as time went on, they had a major improvement in their air pollution, however 99.9% of its citizens are still living in air pollutions at higher than recommended levels.

China encourage <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>cleaner power generation</span> in **households** as well in their **public transportation**.

> [!abstract] Factors affecting air quality in China
> Almost 60% of China's air pollution (*PM2.5*) is from <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>coal burning</span>. They are still the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>worlds largest coal consumer</span> despite increase use of renewable energy.
> 
> There are **challenges in balancing between economic and environment policies**.

Similar to the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-charcoal)'>India case study</span>, not everyone has the same accessibility to more sustainable power sources.

There are also <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>different standpoints in what is important</span>. The **central government** wants to reduce air pollution and want be sustainable. The **regional government** in the cities and provinces want to increase economic growth. **The industry**, state-owned energy companies remain influential over the Ministry of Environmental Protection.

Also because of the **increase in population** which means a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>demand for more resources</span>.
