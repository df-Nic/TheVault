# Accounting Cycle
---
There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>4 steps in the accounting cycle</span>;
## Analysing

The first step is to <span style='color:#f7b731'>analyse financial transactions</span> taken place, this transaction <mark style='background:#f7b731'>must satisfy all of the following</mark> : 
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Exchange in resources</span>
- Transactions conducted at [[Overview of Financial Accounting#Financial Accounting Concepts & Assumptions|arm's-length]] between 2 independent entities
- Reliably measures, usually <span style='color:#f7b731'>dollar wise </span>

If <span style='color:#f7b731'>something has not happened</span> yet such as contract duration, it will only be a valid transaction when the contract starts.
### Collection of Information

Most information comes from <span style='color:#f7b731'>business documents</span> such as; invoices, receipts, purchase orders, supplier invoices.

These documents :
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Provides proof</span> of transaction
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Dollar amount</span> about transaction
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Facilitate and analyse business events</span> due to transactions

Now with technology, <span style='color:#20bf6b'>automation enables efficient</span> and seamless collection of data in volumes.

### Accounting Equation

![[Accounting Equation 1.png|center|500]]

**Equation for equity**
$$\text{Equity} = \text{Capital Stock} + \text{Accumulated other CI} + \text{Retained Earnings}$$
**Where :**
- CI is <span style='color:#f7b731'>comprehensive income</span>

**Equation for retrained earnings**
$$\text{Retain earnings (end)} = \text{Retain earnings (start)} + \text{Revenue} - \text{Expense} - \text{Dividend}$$
**Where :**
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Revenue</span>, everything <span style='color:#f7b731'>earned from sales</span> of goods and services
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Expense</span>, <span style='color:#f7b731'>cost incurred</span> when generating revenue ($\text{Revenue} - \text{Expenses} = \text{Income}$)
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Dividend</span>, any <span style='color:#f7b731'>profits distributed to shareholders</span>

## Recording

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>process of recording</span> is called <mark style='background:#0fb9b1'>journalising</mark>, this involves <mark style='background:#f7b731'>recording of transactions chronologically</mark> using <mark style='background:#0fb9b1'>journal entries</mark> into a document called a <mark style='background:#0fb9b1'>journal</mark>.

Technology such as an <span style='color:#f7b731'>accounting system</span> can computerised or <span style='color:#20bf6b'>automate this process</span>.

### T-Accounts

A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>T-account</span>, will consist of <span style='color:#fa8231'>3 key elements</span> :
1) **Category** / **Account** (Can be anything, assets, loans, tech expenditure)
2) **Debit Side**
3) **Credit Side**

**Example of a T-account :**
![[T-Account Example.png|center|200]]

For <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>assets accounts</span>, <span style='color:#20bf6b'>debit means to increase</span> while <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>credit is to decrease</span>. A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>asset account</span> will typically <span style='color:#f7b731'>have debit balances</span>.

For <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>liability and equity accounts</span>, <span style='color:#20bf6b'>debit means to decrease</span>, while <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>credit is to increase</span>. It will typically <span style='color:#f7b731'>have a credit balance</span>.

For <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>expense and dividends accounts</span>, <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>debit means to increase</span>, while <span style='color:#20bf6b'>credit is to decrease</span>. It will typically <span style='color:#f7b731'>have a debit balance</span>. This is because, under <span style='color:#f7b731'>equity, debit is to decrease</span>, thus for a increase in losses, debit will increase.

For <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>revenue accounts</span>, <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>debit means to decrease</span>, while <span style='color:#20bf6b'>credit is to increase</span>. It will typically <span style='color:#f7b731'>have a credit balance</span>. Similarly, under equity, <span style='color:#f7b731'>credit is to increase in equity</span> and this an increase in revenue means more equity, thus credit is to increase.
## Summarising

Once <span style='color:#fa8231'>all the journal entries are done</span>, the amount will be <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>posted</span> into a <mark style='background:#f7b731'>T-account</mark>. 

This process of <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>posting</span>, is just <span style='color:#f7b731'>copying all the amounts</span> by account and <span style='color:#f7b731'>into the respective columns</span>.

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>final balance of the T-account</span>, will be on the <mark style='background:#f7b731'>side that increases the account</mark>.

Once all the T-accounts are done, the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>trial balance</span> can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>created from the balances of all the T-accounts</span>.
## Reporting

**This step involves :**
1) Prepare adjusting entries
2) Post adjusting entries into <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>T-accounts</span> and get the balance for each account
3) Prepare the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>adjusted trial balance</span> using T-accounts balance
4) Prepare <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>statement of comprehensive income</span> based on trial balance
5) Compute <span style='color:#f7b731'>retained earning</span> balance
6) Prepare <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>balance sheet</span> from trial balance and the retained earnings
7) Prepare statement of **cash flows**
8) Prepare notes to financial statements that provide information about methods and assumptions used
9) Close books for next year
### Accrual Accounting

It a system which allows <span style='color:#f7b731'>revenue to be recorded</span> under certain criteria (**when earned**) <span style='color:#f7b731'>and expenses</span> when incurred to generate revenue (<b><span style='color:#0fb9b1'>matching principle</span></b>), regardless of <span style='color:#f7b731'>weather cash is paid or not</span>.

It <span style='color:#20bf6b'>provides a better measurement of company performance</span> than <span style='color:#fa8231'>cash-basis accounting</span> as it is **recognised only when cash is exchanged**.

However it is important that the **[[Revenue & Receivables|revenue is recognised]]**.

**Why Accrual Accounting**
1) **Shareholders**
	- They require continuous and timely information of the company financial position at the end of the year
	- Determine if a <span style='color:#f7b731'>company's operations succeeds or fails</span> based on statement of comprehensive income
2) **Firm**
	- Requires periodic and timely financial reports to <span style='color:#f7b731'>measure firms performance</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Accrual accounting</span> follows the <b><span style='color:#0fb9b1'>time period-assumption</span></b>, which means that life of a <span style='color:#f7b731'>business is divided into distinct short periods</span> (1 Year) for <span style='color:#f7b731'>timely accounting information</span>, this is known as a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>fiscal</span> or <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>financial year</span>.
### Adjusting Entries

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>4 types of adjusting entries</span> and they are <span style='color:#f7b731'>essential to adjust the accounts</span> at then end of each period. that <b><span style='color:#eb3b5a'>do not involve cash during adjustment</span></b>.

<span style='color:#fa8231'>Each adjusting entry involves</span> one account from :
1) **Balance sheet**
2) **Comprehensive income**

3) **Unrecorded Receivables**
This is considered as an <span style='color:#20bf6b'>asset</span> since the <span style='color:#f7b731'>revenue</span> has been <span style='color:#f7b731'>earned</span> but has <span style='color:#f7b731'>not been paid</span> by the end of the period. It is an asset because, it <span style='color:#f7b731'>shows how much to be collected in the future</span>.

**Adjusting Entry :** <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit accounts receivable</mark></b> or notes if there is interest and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit revenue</mark></b>

2) **Unrecorded Liabilities**
This is considered as an <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>liability</span> since <span style='color:#f7b731'>expenses incurred are not paid are not recorded</span> at the end of that period, it <span style='color:#f7b731'>shows the obligation to pay</span> for the expense in the future.

**Adjusting Entry :** <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit expenses</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit accounts payable</mark></b>

An example : If a company were to pay off a loan every 3 months, then for the 2 months they will record interest as an payable.

3) **Prepaid Expenses**
This is recognised as an <span style='color:#20bf6b'>asset</span> since the <span style='color:#f7b731'>transaction has taken place</span> but the <span style='color:#f7b731'>service</span> has <span style='color:#f7b731'>not</span> been <span style='color:#f7b731'>fulfilled</span>. A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>prepaid expense</span> <span style='color:#f7b731'>reflects the remaining future benefits</span> at the end of the fiscal year.

**Adjusting Entry :**
- *On transaction* -<b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit prepaid expense</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit cash</mark></b>
- *After 1 period (usually 1 month)* - <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit expense</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit prepaid expense</mark></b>. To calculate the amount take $(\text{cash paid } \backslash \text{duration}) \times \text{period length}$

4) **Unearned Revenue**
This is considered as an <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>liability</span> since <span style='color:#f7b731'>cash is received before the delivering of goods and services</span>. This <span style='color:#f7b731'>revenue cannot be recognised</span> at as there is a obligation that has yet to be fulfilled.

<span style='color:#fa8231'>Adjustments are made</span> to <span style='color:#f7b731'>recognised the revenue</span> earned up to that date, which reduces liability and <span style='color:#f7b731'>reflects</span> the <span style='color:#f7b731'>remaining future obligations</span>.

**Adjusting Entry :**
- *On transaction* -<b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit unearned revenue</mark></b>
- *After 1 period (usually 1 month)* - <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit unearned revenue</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit revenue</mark></b>

### Trial Balance
![[Trial Balance Example.png|center|550]]

Everything that is considered <span style='color:#20bf6b'>revenue</span> and <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>expenses</span> will be inside the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>statement of comprehensive income</span>. Which the comprehensive income can be calculated which is $\text{Net income} + \text{Other comprehsnsive income}$.

The <span style='color:#f7b731'>rest will be under the balance sheet</span>, and then calculate retained earning which is $\text{Opening retained earning balance + net income - dividend}$.
### Closing the Books

Based on the trial balance, take <span style='color:#f7b731'>all revenue, expenses and dividend accounts</span> into **retained earning account**. This is to make these accounts also called <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>temporary or nominal accounts</span> to have <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>zero balance</mark></b>. This is known as <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>zeroing</span> and now <span style='color:#f7b731'>expenses and dividend are credit</span> since value is how much will be carried over.

# Role of Technology
---
One key technical aspect of technology is its <span style='color:#20bf6b'>efficiency and error less execution</span>. Thus it <span style='color:#f7b731'>allows automation</span> which <span style='color:#20bf6b'>reduces time spent</span> on doing certain processes in the accounting cycle such as posting journal entries and summarising the accounts into a trial balance.

One down side is that technology's <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>critical thinking skills is not as good as humans</span>. Accountants still need to <span style='color:#f7b731'>analyse companies and transactions, deciding if it needs to be recorded</span>, something <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>too dynamic for technology to decide</span>. Accountants also ensure that <span style='color:#f7b731'>accounting system meets the needs</span> of the company.
