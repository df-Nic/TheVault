# Overview
---
PPE (Property, Plant & Equipment) and IA (Intangible Assets) are reported on the <span style='color:#f7b731'>balance sheet as long-term assets</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Property, Plant & Equipment</span>
>Are tangible, long-lived assets acquired for use in business operations; include land, buildings, machinery, equipment, motor vehicles, ship, aircraft and furniture and fixtures.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Intangible assets</span>
>Are long-lived assets that are used in the operation of a business but do not have physical substance, these include licenses, patents, franchises and goodwill.

# Property, Plant & Equipment
---
The cost of acquiring any PPE is <span style='color:#f7b731'>initially record as a cost</span>, this also <span style='color:#f7b731'>includes</span> any <span style='color:#f7b731'>other cost incurred by getting it ready</span> for its intended use, these include :
- Fright In
- Installation cost
- Repair cost
- Renovations
- Discounts (Reduce its cost/value)

It is only <span style='color:#f7b731'>considered as an asset</span> if and only if :
1) **It is probable that future economic benefits associated with the item will flow to the entity**
2) The cost of the item can be **measured reliably**.
## Acquisition

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>purchasing any PPE by itself</span> :
- **Credit** cash or accounts payable
- **Debit** Equipment / Land / Building / Transportation Equipment (Whatever the asset can be categorised into)

How about when purchasing <span style='color:#fa8231'>more than 1 PPE together</span> for example a building with equipment inside.

First know the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>relative fair market value</span> of the assets. They are used to <span style='color:#f7b731'>determine the respective costs</span> to be assigned to
the acquired assets.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>purchasing any multiple PPE together at a single price</span> :
- Let the fair market values of the land and the building are $1,000,000 and $3,000,000, respectively.
- Let the cost of the asset be $3,600,000
- Cost of land is ${1,000,000}/{4,000,000} \times 3,600,000 = 900,000$
- Cost of building is ${3,000,000}/{4,000,000} \times 3,600,000 = 2,700,000$
- **Credit** cash or accounts payable, $3,600,000
- **Debit** $900,000 for land and $2,700,000 to buildings
## Depreciation

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Depreciation</span>
> Is a **systematic (theoretical) allocation** of the depreciable amount of an asset over its useful life

Depreciation is recorded as a <span style='color:#f7b731'>operating expenses</span> in the SCI and the total <span style='color:#f7b731'>accumulated depreciation</span> of all PPE's are also recorded and it it considered to be a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra-asset against PPE-account</span>.

**Calculating depreciable amount**
$$
\text{Depreciable amount} = \text{Cost} - \text{Redisual Value}
$$
**Where :**
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Residual Value</span> is the estimated <span style='color:#f7b731'>amount obtain from disposal</span> of an asset <span style='color:#f7b731'>after deducting the estimated cost of disposal</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Useful life</span>
>A <span style='color:#f7b731'>period</span> for which an <span style='color:#f7b731'>asset is expected to generate value for the company</span>. Or in other words the number of production to be obtain from the asset 

At the end of the useful life, any balance (**carrying amount**) will become the residual value.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>recording depreciation</span>
- **Debit** depreciation expense
- **Credit** accumulated depreciation `<Item name/description>`

<span style='color:#fa8231'>On the balance sheet</span> accumulated depreciation is recorded as, **Less: accumulated depreciation**.
### Methods to Calculate Depreciation

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>3 methods</span> a company can calculate depreciation :
1) **Straight-Line Method of Depreciation**
2) **Units-of-Production Depreciation**
3) **Diminishing Balance Depreciation**

After choosing a method the company must stick with it unless there is some drastic changes in consumption. And the <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>depreciation amount is usually annually</mark></b>, thus there needs to be a scaling down when calculating for monthly periods.

**Straight-Line Method**
$$
\text{Depreciation Expense} = \frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Estimated Useful Life in Years}}
$$
The above will provide an <span style='color:#f7b731'>annual fixed depreciation rate</span>, thus every year the assets will depreciate at this rate.

The useful life or residual value can change as time continues, this however <span style='color:#f7b731'>only affects future years depreciation</span>. (For this course only for straight line).

Lets say at year $x$ there is a new estimate useful life and new residual value. Then for years $x + 1$ onwards the new depreciation rate will follow these new estimates with the <span style='color:#f7b731'>cost being the residual value from year</span> $\color {#f7b731} {x}$.

**Units-of-production depreciation**
$$
\frac{\text{Cost} - \text{Residual Value}}{\text{Total estimated life in units}} \times \text{Number of units used/produced for the year}
$$
This depreciation method <span style='color:#f7b731'>takes into account how much an asset was used</span> during the year.

This methods is **commonly used for natural resources**, like a goal mine or a plantation. And there is a special term for this called <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>depletion</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Depletion</span>
>The process of cost allocation that assigns the original cost of a natural resource to the periods benefitted.

**Example**
- If a gold mine was purchased at $1,000,000
- Inside is estimated to have 200,000 tons of gold deposits
- Thus the amount per ton is $1,000,000 / $200,000 = $5 per ton
- If 10,000 tons were minded out :
	- **Debit** $10000 \times 5$ in <span style='color:#f7b731'>depletion expense</span>
	- **Credit** the same amount for <span style='color:#f7b731'>accumulated depletion</span>, Gold mine

**Diminishing Balance**
1) *Double Declining-balance (DDB)*
$$
\frac{1}{\text{Estimated life (years)}} \times 2 \times \text{Carrying amount}
$$
**Where**
- $1/\text{Estimated life (years)} \times2$ is the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>DDB rate</span> in <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>percentage</mark></b>. This rate is constant throughout
- Carrying amount is $\text{Cost} - \text{Accumulated depreciation}$

One thing to <span style='color:#f7b731'>take note</span> of is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>residual and the carrying amount</span> as the carrying amount cannot be below residual value. Thus the depreciation expense will be $ex = \text{Carring amount} - \text{Residual amount}$.
## Impairments

These happen when there is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>decline in value of a long term asset</span> dues to events such as natural disasters, war etc.

Companies will do an impairment test to find its <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>recoverable amount</span>, which is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>higher of an assets net fair value and its value in use</span>. Another word for value in use is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>present value of cash flows</span>.

If the <span style='color:#fa8231'>recoverable amount is smaller than the carrying amount</span>, then the <span style='color:#f7b731'>impairment loss is recognised</span> as a <span style='color:#f7b731'>non-operating expense</span> in the income statement, $\text{Impairment loss} = \text{Carrying amount} - \text{Recoverable amount}$.

Accumulated impairment loss is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra asset account</span> on the balanced sheet against PPE. While this and accumulated depreciation are <span style='color:#f7b731'>both reported in the balance sheet as carrying amount</span>.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>there is impairment loss</span> :
- **Debit** Impairment Loss 
- **Credit** Accumulated Impairment loss, `<Asset category>`
## Disposal

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>2 ways</span> to dispose of a PPE :
1) Discarding or Scrapping
2) Selling

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>gain or loss from disposal</span> is calculated as such :
$$
\text{Sales Proceeds} - \text{Cost of Disposal} - \text{Carring Amount}
$$
This is reported as a <span style='color:#f7b731'>non-operating gain or loss</span> in the **SCI**.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>discarding an asset</span> :
- **Debit** Accumulated Depreciation `<Item>` and <span style='color:#f7b731'>Loss</span> on Disposal of `<item>`, it there is any losses
- **Credit** asset item, cash if there is any cost and <span style='color:#f7b731'>Gain</span> on disposal of `<item>` is there is any gains

# Intangible Assets
---
it is an <span style='color:#f7b731'>identifiable non-monetary asset</span> without physical substance.

Are rights and privileges that are long-lived, not held for resale, have no physical substance and providing owner with competitive advantage over other firms. For example, patent, trademark, goodwill

<span style='color:#fa8231'>Internally generated intangible assets</span> are <span style='color:#f7b731'>not</span> recognised on the balance sheet, while <span style='color:#fa8231'>acquired intangible assets</span> are <span style='color:#f7b731'>reported and valued at the transaction price</span> through an [[Overview of Financial Accounting#Financial Accounting Concepts & Assumptions|arm's length transaction]].

If there is a <span style='color:#fa8231'>merger</span>, then the company can report the intangible assets from merging.

**Types of intangible assets**
1) **Patent**
It is an exclusive right granted by a national government that enables an inventor to control over a period of time the manufacture, sale, or use of an invention or design.

The cost of a patent includes purchase price, filing and registry fees (acquired patent only, recognised as an intangible asset). Internally generated patent is not recognised as an intangible asset.

The expenditures during the research stage are not included. (**expense off as incurred**)

2) **Trademark**
A distinctive name, symbol, or slogan that distinguishes a product or service from similar products or services.

Internally Generated trademark (generated by the company) are not recognized on the balance sheet. <span style='color:#f7b731'>Only acquired trademark is reported on the balance sheet</span>.

3) **Copyrights**
An exclusive right that permits an author to sell, license, or control his/her work over a period of time.

it is reported in the balance sheet if bought valued at the transaction price.

4) **Franchises**
An entity that has been licensed to sell the product or to offer a particular service in a given area.

The recorded cost of the franchise includes any sum paid specifically for the franchise right as well as legal fees and other costs incurred in obtaining it.

5) License
It is just another form of franchise.

6) **Goodwill**
An intangible asset that exists when a <span style='color:#f7b731'>business is valued at more than the fair market value</span> of its net assets (**total assets – total liabilities**), usually due to strategic location, reputation, good customer relations, or similar factors; equal to the excess of the <span style='color:#f7b731'>purchase price over the fair market value of the net assets purchased</span>.

**Recognized only when it is purchased as part of the acquisition of another company**.

Basically goodwill happens when the price to buy the company is <span style='color:#f7b731'>greater</span> than the market value of the company.

## Amortisation of Intangible Assets

It is the systematic allocation of the <span style='color:#f7b731'>depreciable amount of an intangible asset</span> over its useful life. With it <span style='color:#f7b731'>cost</span> being the <span style='color:#f7b731'>amount of cash or cash equivalent paid</span> to acquire it.

Note that straight-line amortisation is generally used. Generally assumed to have no residual value.

Intangibles with an <span style='color:#f7b731'>indefinite life</span> like goodwill or a license being extended indefinitely <span style='color:#f7b731'>will not be amortised</span>.

To <span style='color:#fa8231'>calculate amortisation expense</span> on finite life intangibles are :
$$
\text{Armotisation expense} = \frac{\text{Cost}}{\text{Useful Life}}
$$
And the <span style='color:#f7b731'>accumulated amortisation is a contra asset</span> account to intangible assets.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>recording depreciation</span>
- **Debit** amortisation expense <`item name/description`>
- **Credit** accumulated amortisation `<Item name/description>`

There is also impairment which must be calculated every year

# Capitalise & Expense
---
General rule is to <span style='color:#f7b731'>capitalize if the expenditure will bring long term economic benefits</span> to the entity (more than 1 year duration). if its capitalise then the value will be **debited** into the respective PPE account.

An example of <span style='color:#fa8231'>expensing a expenditure</span>, is through repairing or maintenance. Cost incurred maintain the productive capacity of an asset and <span style='color:#f7b731'>not increase the productive life, usefulness or capacity</span>. This will be <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>expense off</span>, meaning they are <span style='color:#f7b731'>reported as an operating expense</span> in SCI.

An example of <span style='color:#fa8231'>capitalising an expenditure</span>, is for example equipment upgrades. Which <span style='color:#f7b731'>can increase the productive life, usefulness or capacity of the assets</span>. This cost will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>capitalised</span> and <span style='color:#f7b731'>depreciated</span> over the remaining life of the asset.

As for improvements to land they are usually permanent and are <span style='color:#f7b731'>capitalised as part of the land account</span>. 

## Land

Those that have <span style='color:#f7b731'>limited useful life</span> likes roads are <span style='color:#f7b731'>capitalised under a separate land improvement account</span> and depreciated over their useful lives (Usually **shorter than the useful life of land**).

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Freehold land</span>
>Land that is <span style='color:#f7b731'>not depreciated</span> as it last indefinitely as the ownership is indefinite

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Leasehold land</span>
>It <span style='color:#f7b731'>has a useful life</span> in terms of its lease (example, 99 years for Singapore HDB).

## Research & Development

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Research</span>
>An activity undertaken to discover new knowledge that will be useful in developing new products, services, or processes

Research costs are defined as those R&D costs<span style='color:#f7b731'> incurred before</span> technological feasibility has been established.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Development</span>
>Is the application of research findings or other knowledge to a plan or design for the production of new or substantially improved materials, devices, products, processes, systems or services before the start of commercial production or use

Development costs are R&D cost <span style='color:#f7b731'>incurred after</span> technological feasibility has been established. These <span style='color:#f7b731'>cost are capitalised</span>.

## Advertising

In general any cost incurred from <span style='color:#f7b731'>advertising are considered as expense</span>. This is because the <span style='color:#f7b731'>future benefits are uncertain</span>.

However these <span style='color:#fa8231'>are not the case for some</span> specialised scenarios :
1) If the advertisement <span style='color:#f7b731'>targets customer who have purchased</span> products in the past
2) If the advertisement is characterised by the ability to <span style='color:#f7b731'>estimate how many customers will respond favourably</span>
