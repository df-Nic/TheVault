# Experimental Study
---
Side not, no matter how <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>perfect the study is done</span>, it <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>does not mean it can be generalisable</span>, there are other factors like sampling technique, size.

It is also known as a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>controlled experiment</span> or just <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>experiment</span>. It <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>manipulates a variable to cause an effect on another</span>.

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>primary goal</span> or usage is to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>provide a cause and effect relationship</span> between the independent and dependent variable.

There are usually <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>2 groups</span> in an experiment :
1) **Treatment group** - The group which will receive the treatment
2) **Control group** - The group that will not receive the treatment

**Both groups are needed** as without one, there is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>no baseline for comparison</span> (**Usually compare with control group**).

For any experiment **when establishing a cause and effect relationship**, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>only 1 variable is independent</span>, the rest must be constant (fixed). 

For **example**, if the experiment is to see if coffee can help pass a exam. Then the study time, test date must be fixed.

But i can be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>hard to find people with similar aspects</span>. Once way to remove variability from variables is to do <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>random assignment</span>, which involves <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>randomly assigning people into the control group</span> with no replacement.

This works as **long as the number of people are large**, the groups form will <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>tend to be similar in all aspects</span>.
## Blinding
This **does not mean** the **control group can be left alone** in an experiment as it can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>cause biases</span> as they <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>might do other things which can affect the dependent variable</span>. 

One way is to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>use a placebo</span> to give the illusion that they are being tested on. However this may give rise to the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>placebo effect</span> where the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>placebo might show positive effects on the control group</span>, even though it does nothing.

To solve this issue, a technique called <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>blinding</span> can be used :
1) **Subject blinding**
	> <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Subjects will not know which group they are in</span>, also the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>placebo used will be very similar to the treatment</span> given to the other. Preventing personal beliefs about the treatment.

2) **Assessor blinding**
	>The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>assessors will not know which group they are observing</span>. This is also to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>prevent assessor bias</span> when making observations. 

Having both in an experiment is known as a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>double blinded</span> experiment.
# Observational Study
---
This is usually used when there are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ethical concerns</span>. As researches <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>cannot manipulate one variable</span> thus, it <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>does not provide convincing evidence</span> of a **cause and effect relationship**.

There will <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>still be treatment and control groups</span> or they can be known as exposure and non-exposure.

Unlike experimental studies, subjects<span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'> own self assign themselves</span> which group they will be in.

This does not mean that this is not useful, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>observational studies</span> can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>provide evidence of association</span>.
