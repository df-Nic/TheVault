# Inequality
---
There are **2 main types of inequality economic and social**. However they are actually <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>closely linked</span></b>.

Economic inequality is connected to various other forms of inequality in society like:
- Gender
- Age
- Education
- Citizenship
- Visa Status
- "Class"

> [!note] Computation of inequality
> The <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Gini coefficient</span></b> is a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>quantitative way of measuring one's inequality</span></b> (*0 for perfect equality, 1 for perfect inequality*).

**Inequality today**, has been <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>growing</span></b>. Even with rapid economic growth, it is <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>clearly not evenly distributed</span></b>.

We always think that inequality affects economies and material conditions of humans but it <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>can also affect culture</span></b> (*Parasite, squid game which addresses the gulf between the rich & poor in South Korea*). Not only that but it can <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>change political behavior</span></b> (*Different parities will address the issue*).

> [!info] Intersectionality
> <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Intersectionality theory</span></b> explores how the differential situatedness of social agents influences the ways they affect and are affected by various social, economic, and political projects.
> 
> In other words <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>we are defined by more than one aspect of our identity</span></b> (* minority women might have different experience than a minority male*).

> [!info] Class
> Modern usage emerged in 18th and 19th centuries during industrial revolution, <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>defining social hierarchies along economic lines</span></b> (*rather than religious/legal/etc*).
> 
> It can focus on one's <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>economic</span></b> (*Who owns assets, the elites or who owns nothing but only can work*) standing or <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>social</span></b> standing (*Education, social network, taste, consumption patterns*).

> [!info] Precarity
> A state of being defined by its <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>insecurity and vulnerability</span></b>. It tends to be used to refer to people who are **unemployed, underemployed, or insecurely employed** (*job insecurity*).
> 
> It is a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>sense of anxiety of your own position to society</span></b>. Which affects their behaviour and choices.

## Inequality in a Middle Class Society in Japan

Japan has a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>aging population</span></b>, but how did we get here. After the world war, <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Japan was occupied by the US</span></b>.

There were <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>protests against the US</span></b> due to the many **political decisions and policies made**.

After the 1960s a new liberal democratic party prime minister **Ikeda Hayato** went on a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>more gentler approach by rapidly developing the economy</span></b>, <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>reducing tensions and helping everyone</span></b>.

However there was a **asset price bubble**, where high levels of <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>consumption and speculation, encouraged by state policy</span></b>. And in the 1990s the **bubble popped** and caused a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>long economic recession</span></b>.

> [!abstract] Family-corporate system
> The **lifestyle norm** in of a middle class Japanese person. Males get hired by large corporations (*sararīman*), the females will be housewives (*senmon shufu*) while the children's academic results is a sense of self worth.
> 
> <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Men will have very little time to interact with the family</span></b> because of this idea of working hard.
> 
> As for consumption, they aim to have a large home in the suburbs, own a car and many more.

In reality:
- Many men **did not have well paying jobs** (*Since the lifetime employment system was for men*)
- **Women need to work but only can get part time jobs** because of the gap of having a child and getting married
- **Children has extreme pressure on doing very well to school**. If they do not do well then their sense of worth is eroded.

Collapse of the postwar “family-corporate system”

**Economic and policy changes** since the 1990s have drastically <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>reduced the number of people who can pursue “normal” careers</span></b> in Japan.

**Precarious employment** has led many to believe that they are <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>not secure enough to get married and start a family</span></b> for the foreseeable future.

Inability to pursue a “normal” life leads to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>loss of hope, isolation, the various social breakdowns</span></b> (*Hikikomori / NEET*) that Japanese media has been obsessed with since the 1990s.
- Perhaps they can find that future in new forms of social connection beyond the “family-corporate system”?
