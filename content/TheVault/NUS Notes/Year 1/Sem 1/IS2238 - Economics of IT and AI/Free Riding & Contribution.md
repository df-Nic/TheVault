# Type of Goods
---
**Excludable**
> Goods whose <span style='color:#f7b731'>ownership or access can be restricted</span>

People can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>prevented</span> from using these goods if they have not paid from having access to it

**Rivalrous**
> Supply is limited and each <span style='color:#f7b731'>purchase diminishes the amount</span> available to another person

## Private Goods

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Typical</span> goods or services that are <mark class="hltr-orange">both</mark> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>excludable</span> and <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>rivalrous</span>

These include; Train Tickets, Taxi Rides, Plane Tickets, House, Car
## Public Goods

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Collective</span> goods or services that are <mark class="hltr-orange">both not</mark> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>excludable</span> and <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>rivalrous</span>

This type of good can lead to the <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>free-rider problem</span>

These include; Air, Sunshine, Education, Security
### Digital Goods

These type of goods can be classified under <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>public goods</span> 

1) It availability does not get affected with one's consumption as it can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>duplicated infinitely with negligible cost</span>
2) Freely available public goods like e-books can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>considered non-excludable</span>
	 Note that there are <span style='color:#f7b731'>intellectual property laws</span> like copyright and patent laws to protect creator's rights and make goods excludable. Also digital players can charge <span style='color:#f7b731'>subscription fees </span>(service fees) from customers
## Common Goods

They are goods or services that are <span style='color:#f7b731'>not</span> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>excludable</span> but <span style='color:#f7b731'>are</span> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>rivalrous</span>, usually its a <span style='color:#f7b731'>common poll of resources </span>

For example, if someone fishes something up, the person owns that fish and there will be less supply

These include; Forest, Mines, Fisheries, Clean Water

## Club Goods

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Artificially scarce</span> goods or services that are <span style='color:#f7b731'>are</span> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>excludable</span> but <span style='color:#f7b731'>not</span> <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>rivalrous</span>

Supply does not get affected even though it is <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>excludable</span> but <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>it might happen if congestion occurs</span>

These include; Netflix, Television Channels, Cinemas, Wireless Internet

# Free Riding
---
Many online communities still exist is due to the <span style='color:#f7b731'>importance of contribution from their users</span> in forms of;
- <mark class="hltr-orange">Content Generation</mark> where users contribute things like posts, comments, videos and reviews (YouTube Instagram)
- <mark class="hltr-orange">Knowledge Sharing</mark> where people can asks questions can other can share their knowledge by proving answers (Stack Overflow, Reddit)

This gave rise to something that is known as <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>free-rider problem</span>. It is a type of <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>market failure</span> where <span style='color:#f7b731'>people benefit from public goods</span> or services of a communal nature that <span style='color:#f7b731'>do not pay for them or underpay</span>

**Tragedy of the Commons**
> Individuals <span style='color:#f7b731'>act on their own self-interest </span>and <span style='color:#f7b731'>deplete</span> or <span style='color:#f7b731'>degrade</span> a shared resource (Selfish behaviour)

The main issue is that <span style='color:#f7b731'>people let others pay</span>, and there is an <span style='color:#f7b731'>inability to exclude non-players</span>

For example, a open-source software but only a few contribute back, might halt the improvement or evolution leading to market failure due to insufficient contribution

## Pareto Principle

Also known as the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>80-20 principle</span>

Underproduction / Overconsumption is a major challenge for many websites and communities. Thus they rely on people who <span style='color:#f7b731'>contribute heavily</span>

For example, large portion of sales comes from a small population of customers

## 1% Rule

The in digital economy, about <span style='color:#f7b731'>1% of users create content while 99% just consumes</span> it

Breakdown is that
- 90% of the users are lurkers
- 9% of the users contribute from time to time, but other priorities dominate their time
- 1% of users participate a lot and account from the most contributions

The <span style='color:#f7b731'>proliferation</span> of social media in the recent past has provided end users a powerful platform to voice their opinions. But, <span style='color:#f7b731'>1% of users participate a lot and account for most contributions</span>
### Downsides of Inequality

This downside, implies that such <span style='color:#f7b731'>inequities</span> may <span style='color:#f7b731'>give a bias understanding </span>as majority of the users are silent

In the **Business Perspective**
- Reviews or feedback only represent a minority of the people compared to the whole sample

In the **Social Perspective**
- In Politics If a party nominates a candidate supported by the "netroots," it may lose because such candidates' positions will be too extreme to appeal to mainstream voters . Postings on political blogs come from less than 0.1% of voters.

## Overcoming Free-Rider Problem

Overcoming this problem can be beneficial;

**Social Importance**
> If the problem persist it can <span style='color:#f7b731'>amplify social inequities </span>

**Business Importance**
> In a business stand point it can <span style='color:#f7b731'>undermine revenue models</span>. If people where to get a something for free, it <span style='color:#f7b731'>disincentivizes spending</span>, leading to revenue losses, in addition businesses might <span style='color:#f7b731'>obtain skewed perspective of their actual customer base</span>

If business only <span style='color:#f7b731'>considers</span> the feedback or buying habits of <span style='color:#f7b731'>paying customers</span> and ignores free free-riders, they might <span style='color:#f7b731'>not accurately understand the market's broader willingness to pay or potential price sensitivities</span>

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Free-riders</span>, even though they might not be directly paying or engaging in official channels, <span style='color:#f7b731'>could have valuable feedback or differing preferences</span>

### Reward / Incentives

It is the most <span style='color:#f7b731'>fundamental and important </span>mechanism and they should take into account the <span style='color:#f7b731'>characteristics of motivation</span>

Motivation can be either <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>intrinsic</span> (Personal desire) or <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>extrinsic</span> (external benefit or reward)

|                       Intrinsic                        |               Extrinsic                |
|:------------------------------------------------------:|:--------------------------------------:|
|     Reading a book because you enjoy story telling     |       Reading a book for a test        |
|     Exercising because you want to relieve stress      |       Exercising to lose weight        |
| Cleaning the house because it helps you feel organized | Cleaning the house because of visitors | 

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Intrinsic</span> motivation lasts in the long run but is challenging to inspire
	 Here physical rewards or excessive rewards may <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>not be appropriate </span>

<span style='color:#3867d6'>Motivational crowding out perspective</span> argues that incentives can bring <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>impairment of self determination</span> to people intrinsic motivation, by which <span style='color:#f7b731'>incentives can reduce agents' perceived autonomy and cause them to disengage</span> (Making something less intrinsic or <span style='color:#f7b731'>impairment of self-perception</span>)

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Incentives</span> are <mark class="hltr-orange">less persuasive on people with high intrinsic motivation</mark>, while incentives are <mark class="hltr-orange">more effective</mark> in persuading individuals have <mark class="hltr-orange">low intrinsic motivation</mark>

Motivation of the customers, affects the way customers respond to rewards and incentives. If people are motivated, then there is no need to provide incentives. <span style='color:#f7b731'>Focus on the people who are less motivated to retain them</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Extrinsic</span> Motivation tends to be short lived but easier to ignite

Example: **ML-Nudging**, where using historical data, <span style='color:#f7b731'>provide incentives on low days</span>
### Gamification

It is the application of <span style='color:#f7b731'>game-design elements and principles on a non-gaming context</span>, this includes badges, levels. leader boards, virtual currency

It helps <span style='color:#f7b731'>enhance learning motivation</span> and <span style='color:#f7b731'>reduce boredom and unproductiveness</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Gamification</span> also taps into the innate human <span style='color:#f7b731'>desire for achievement</span>, competition and recognition. By presenting tasks as challenges with rewards for motivation

**Important Aspects of Gamification**
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Instant Feedback</span>, be it through points, badges, progress bars, allows users to know how they are doing immediately which can be encouraging and rewarding
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>Progression</span>, gives a sense of accomplishment, motivating users to contribute more to advance further

Example: Reddit and their coins and awards and Twitch gamification features  
### Social Comparison

In psychology, social comparison theory suggests that people <span style='color:#f7b731'>value their own personal and social worth</span> by assessing how they <span style='color:#f7b731'>compare to others</span>

The authors design a series of <span style='color:#f7b731'>online performance feedback interventions</span> that aim to motivate the production of user generated content (UGC).

<span style='color:#3867d6'>Competitively framed feedback</span> is most effective at motivating male subjects

Example: Badges, a gold badges are the most influential in motivation of users while bronze badges are least impactful. This badge levels increase the amount of subsequent answering activity
