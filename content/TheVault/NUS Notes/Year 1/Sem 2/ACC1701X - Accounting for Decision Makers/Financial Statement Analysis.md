# What is it For
---
It allows to examine the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>relationship and trends</span> between financial statement numbers over time. It evaluates past performances and predict future ones and comparing with other companies or benchmarking.

**Who needs the FSA**
- **Investors :** For investment decisions
- **Creditors :** For lending decisions
- **Management :** For business strategic and tactical decisions

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>Limitations</span> of a financial statement analysis :
- <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Does not have all relevant information</span> like customer satisfaction and operational data
- <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Hard to compare between different companies</span>, due to different accounting methods used
- <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Cannot solve companies strategic and tactical issues</span> but it can point out areas to gather more information
- Focuses on historical data which may cause users to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>overlook current important information</span>
# Financial Statement Analysis Methods
---
## Vertical Analysis

It is also known as the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>common-size financial statements</span>.

If it is for :
1) **Statement of comprehensive income** : Then all amounts/items will be as a <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>percentage of net sales</mark></b>

It allows <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>comparisons</span> :

- **Across companies** in the same industry of different sizes

# Financial Ratios
---
## Liquidity Ratios

**Liquidity**
>It measures a company’s ability to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>pay its debts in the short run</span>.

To <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>measure the liquidity</span> of a business the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>current ratio</span> is used :
$$
\text{Current Ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets}}{\text{Current Liabilitie}}
$$
**Where :**
- Values used are <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>year end balances</mark></b>
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>higher the better</span>, but if its <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>too high</span>, then it means the company is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>not utilising their assets properly</span>

To measure the<span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'> immediate short-term liquidity</span> of a business the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-blue)'>acid-test (quick) ratio</span> is used :
$$
\text{Acid-test ratio} = \frac{\text{Current Assets} - \text{Inventories} - \text{Pre-Payments}}{\text{Current Liabilities}}
$$
**Where :**
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>higher the value the better</span> the liquidity
## Efficiency Ratios

**Efficiency**
>Is how fast they can covert inventory or receivables into cash. It measures how successfully a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>company uses its assets</span> or how much <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>income</span> a company can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>generate</span> with its asset.

Measuring <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>efficiency of PPE</span> by calculating sales generated per dollar spent on PPE, is called the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>fixed asset (PPE) turnover</span> :
$$
\text{Fixed asset (PPE) turover} = \frac{\text{Net Sales}}{\text{Average Net PPE}}
$$
**Where**
- **Average net PPE** is calculated by $(PPE_{y1} + PPE_{y2})/2$ and the value is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>after depreciation</span>, where $y_{1}$ is the current year and $y_{2}$ is the year before
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>higher the value the better the turnover</span>, meaning $1 of PPE generates a lot of sales.

Sometimes it is good to calculate the number of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>days to inventory to turn into cash</span>, this is called the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>operating cycle</span> :
$$
\text{Operating cycle days} = \text{Days to sell inventory once purchaced} + \text{Average collection period}
$$
**Where :**
- It it just summing up <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>how many days</span> it needs when <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>inventory</span> is being <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>purchased</span> to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>cash being collected</span> form sales
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>lower the value the better</span> since it means that sales to cash happens very fast

What if inventory is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>purchased on account</span>, then use the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>number of days purchases in accounts payable</span> :
$$
\text{Number of days purchases in accounts payable} = \frac{365}{\text{Net Purchases}} \times \text{Average AP}
$$
$$
\text{Purchaces turnover} = \frac{\text{Net purchases}}{\text{Average AP}}
$$

If operating cycle <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>requires financing</span> :
$$
\text{Operating cycle} = \text{Operating Cycle Days} - \text{Number of Days' Purchases in Accounts Payable}
$$

How to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>reduce operating cycle</span> :
- Shorten the collection period
- Reduce the number of days' sales in inventory (Inventory management, data analytics)
- Lengthen the payable duration (Longer credit period), this can cause distrust with supply and to solve this companies can source from less suppliers

## Solvency Ratios

**Solvency**
>Is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ability to relay all liabilities both long and short term</span>. Low solvency means companies cannot meet financial obligations and can be forced into bankruptcy.

To measure the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>leverage or debt </span> of a company, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>debt ratio</span> can be used :
$$
\text{Debit ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Liabilities}}{\text{Total Assets}}
$$
**Where :**
- Values used are <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>year end balances</mark></b>
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>higher ratio, the higher the solvency risk</span>, but if its <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>too low</span>, then it means the company is not getting loans to further improve their business 

To measure the dollar value of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>borrowed funds per dollar invested by owners</span>, use the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>debt-to-equity-ratio</span> :
$$
\text{Debt-to-equity ratio} = \frac{\text{Total Liabilities}}{\text{Total Equity}}
$$
- Values used are <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>year end balances</mark></b>
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>higher ratio, the higher the solvency risk</span>
- If the value is 1, then the amount borrowed is equal to the amount invested

To measure the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>borrower's ability to make interest payments</span>, use the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>time interest earned</span> :
$$
\text{Times interest earned} = \frac{\text{Income Before Interest \& Taxes (Operating Profit)}}{\text{Annual Interest Expense}}
$$

The above <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>measures how much buffer</span> a company has to make periodic interest payments, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher the ratio</span> the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>lower the risk of insolvency</span>.

## Profitability Ratio

**Profitability** 
>Is measures gross profit, return of asset or the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>companies operating success</span>

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>net income for each dollar sales</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>profit margin</span> :
$$
\text{Profit margin (Return on sales)} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Net Sales}}
$$
The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher</span> the value the<span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'> better the return on sales</span>.

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>companies asset profitability</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>return on assets</span> :
$$
\text{Return on assets} = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}
$$
**Where :**
- Average total assets is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>average asset at the beginning and at the end</span>
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher</span> the value, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>more profit the assets are generating</span>

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>companies efficiency</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>asset turnover</span> :
$$
\text{Asset turnover} = \frac{\text{Net Sales}}{\text{Average Total Assets}}
$$
**Where :**
- Average total assets is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>average asset at the beginning and at the end</span>
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher</span> the value, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>more sales the assets are generating</span>

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>profit over per dollar of owner investment</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>return on equity</span> :
$$
\text{Return on equity} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Perference Dividends}}{\text{Average Total Equity}}
$$
**Where :**
- Average total equity is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>average equity at the beginning and at the end</span>

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>net income earned on each ordinary share</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>earnings per share</span> (EPS) :
$$
\text{EPS} = \frac{\text{Net Income} - \text{Perference Dividends}}{\text{Average Number of Outstanding Ordinary Shares}}
$$
**Where :**
- Average outstanding ordinary shares is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>average outstanding ordinary shares at the beginning and at the end</span>

To measure <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>growth potential, earning stability and management capabilities</span>, use <span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>price-earnings ratio</span> (PE ratio) :
$$
\text{PE ratio} = \frac{\text{Market Value of Shares}}{\text{Net Income}} = \frac{\text{Price Per Share}}{\text{Earnings Per Share}}
$$
## Cash Flow Ratios

These ratios are used when <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>analysis</span> of income statement <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>does not provide</span> a clear picture of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>economic performances</span> :
1) Large non cash expenses, impairment loss and depreciation might not be reflected
2) Rapid growth, high growth does not mean sufficient cash flows
3) <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Window dressing time</span>, looking financially strong but in reality they are not

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>Cash flow to net income</span>, this ratio <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>highlights differences</span> between <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>cash flow</span> from <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>operations</span> and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>net income</span> :
$$
\frac{\text{Cash Flow from Operations}}{\text{Net Income}}
$$
**Where :**
- If the ratio is close to 1, then <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>net income is well-supported by operating cash flow</span>
- Any <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>significant change</span> can indicate accounting assumptions and manipulations in <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>window-dressing the higher net income</span>

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-teal)'>Cash flow adequacy</span>, shows a companies capability to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>finance its expansion</span> through cash from operations <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>without external funding</span> :
$$
\frac{\text{Cash Flow from Operations}}{\text{Cash Paid for Capital Expenditures}}
$$
**Where :**
- The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher</span> the ratio the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>higher the ability for a company</span> to be able to fund its capital expansion

# DuPont Framework
---
It is to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>break down the return on equity</span> to 3 ratios :
1) **Return on sales**
2) **Asset turnover**
3) **Assets-to-equity ratio**

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>Use</span> this framework when :
1) Need to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>identify factors</span> that cause ROE to change
2) Provide insights of companies areas of strength and weakness in terms of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>profitability</span>, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>efficiency</span> and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>leverage</span>

![[DuPont Framework Equation.png|center]]

**Return on sales** is interpreted as the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>profit generated</span> from each dollar of sales.

**Asset turnover** is interpreted as the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>sales generated</span> by each dollar of assets.

**Asset-to-equity ratio** is interpreted as the dollars of assets for each <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>dollar invested by stockholders</span>.
