# Purpose of Financial Accounting
---
Think of accounting as a type of <span style='color:#f7b731'>bookkeeping</span> for financial data and data evaluation. A <mark style='background:#f7b731'>standard to track, measure and report business performance</mark>. 

Its "books" provides financial information to other but <mark style='background:#f7b731'>primarily for external users</mark> (Lenders & Investors) to <span style='color:#f7b731'>help make economic decisions</span>.

Its sole purpose is to <mark style='background:#f7b731'>accumulate, measure and communicate</mark> financial information about a business.
> Business activities $\rightarrow$ Financial statements $\rightarrow$ Communication to users
## Functionality

There are <span style='color:#f7b731'>3 main purposes</span> for an <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>accounting system</span> :
1) **Analysis**
	<span style='color:#f7b731'>Analyse business events</span> and determine if information should be captured by the system (What).
2) **Bookkeeping**
	Daily routine of <span style='color:#f7b731'>tracking transactions</span> (How).
3) **Reporting & Validation**
	Use information to <span style='color:#f7b731'>evaluate</span> the financial health and performance of <span style='color:#f7b731'>the business</span> (Why).
## Users

There are 2 types of users <mark style='background:#f7b731'>external and internal users</mark>.
### External
| User | Purpose |
| :--: | :--: |
| Lenders / Creditors | Access companies credit risk |
| Investors | Evaluate risk and returns for investing in a comapny |
| Competitors | Evaluate competitiveness of company (Market share, profitability) |
| Suppliers | Access companies ability to pay |
| Customers | Access continuity of business |
| Government Agencies | Ensure compliance to prevailing laws and regulations |
| Media | Report on current affairs and matters |
Since a companies' <span style='color:#f7b731'>financial records are public</span>, they might <span style='color:#f7b731'>not disclose too much important information</span> to <span style='color:#20bf6b'>prevent their competitors from gaining a competitive advantage</span>. 
### Internal
| User | Purpose |
| :--: | :--: |
| Exmployees | Access performance for bonuses, long term promises like pensions and retirement benefits |
| Management | Evaluate company performance, find areas of improvement through <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>management accounting</span> |
| Labour Union | Negotiate employment contracts with management and ensure employees well being |
There is a difference between <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>financial and management accounting</span>. 

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Financial accounting</span> : Provides <mark style='background:#f7b731'>financial information for external users</mark>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Management accounting</span> : Provides information (Cost analysis, divisional performance) to <mark style='background:#f7b731'>internal users</mark> like staff and management to <mark style='background:#f7b731'>improve business performance</mark>.

<mark style='background:#0fb9b1'>Accountant</mark> : Measures and report financial results to <mark style='background:#f7b731'>advise management on how to achieve business goals</mark>.

# Standards of Financial Accounting
---
To <span style='color:#fa8231'>uphold the quality and standard</span> for financial reporting, came along various standards of financial accounting :
1) **International financial reporting standards (IFRS)**
>Set by the <span style='color:#fa8231'>International Accounting Standard Board (IASB)</span>, it is internationally adopted by 147/168 jurisdictions (87%) includes Singapore's financial reporting standards.
2) **Generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP)**
>Used by the United states. 
3) **Chinese Accounting Standards (CAS)**
> Used by China.

These <span style='color:#f7b731'>standards should be globally followed</span> to achieve harmonisation, so as to <mark style='background:#20bf6b'>ensure consistency, save time, standardisation</mark>.

---
There are <span style='color:#f7b731'>5 principles of behaviours</span> for an accountant :
1) Integrity
2) Objectivity
3) Professional competence and due care
4) Confidentiality
5) Professional behaviour

To help guide accountants on ethical dilemmas, the <span style='color:#fa8231'>institute of Singapore charted accountants (ISCA)</span>, issued the <span style='color:#f7b731'>code of professional conduct and ethics</span>. All in the hopes of <span style='color:#20bf6b'>preventing dishonestly and tempering of data</span>.
## Role of Auditors

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>2 types of auditors</span>, they are <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>internal and external auditors</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Internal auditors</span> : <mark style='background:#f7b731'>Evaluate adequacy</mark> on internal controls and <mark style='background:#f7b731'>encourage adherence</mark> to internal controls.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>External auditors</span> : Examine and determine if <mark style='background:#f7b731'>financial statements follow the accounting standards</mark> and issue <mark style='background:#f7b731'>opinions about reliability of organisation's internal controls</mark>.

<span style='color:#20bf6b'>Benefits of having good auditors</span>, is to present a good quality financial report. This can : 
- **Managers**, want favourable results to be able to, <span style='color:#f7b731'>obtain loans</span>, receive <span style='color:#f7b731'>more bonuses</span> and <span style='color:#f7b731'>higher stock prices</span> for options / grants
- **External Auditors**, have economic incentives to perform good audits like, <span style='color:#f7b731'>reputation and prevent lawsuits</span>
- **Investors**, rely on audited FA <span style='color:#f7b731'>to buy / sell shares</span>
- **Lenders**, rely on audited FA to <span style='color:#f7b731'>evaluate credit risks</span>

# Financial Accounting Concepts & Assumptions
---
1) **Separate entity concept**
>Activities of a business entity are <span style='color:#f7b731'>separated</span> from those of the individual owners.

2) **Time-period assumption**
>It assumes that the long life of a company can be <span style='color:#f7b731'>reported in a shorter time period</span> (Annually, Quarterly, Monthly, Fiscal Year).

3) **Arm's-length transactions assumption**
><span style='color:#f7b731'>Dealings</span> between businesses and entities are <span style='color:#f7b731'>conducted rationally</span> and all parties are acting for their <span style='color:#f7b731'>own interests</span>.

4) **Cost Principle**
> Financial statements are measured at their <span style='color:#f7b731'>historical or original cost</span> at transaction. It is assumed to represent the fair market at transaction.

5) **Fair value principle**
>Assets & Liabilities should be <span style='color:#f7b731'>measured at fair value</span>, <span style='color:#f7b731'>improving relevancy</span> of accounting information.

6) **Monetary measurement**
> <span style='color:#f7b731'>Items</span> in financial statements must be <span style='color:#f7b731'>measurable in dollar value</span>.

7) **Going concern assumption**
><span style='color:#f7b731'>Entities are assumed to be sustainable</span> for the foreseeable future (<span style='color:#20bf6b'>no liquidation</span>) when preparing financial statements. If <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>under liquidation</span>, assets of a company are valued lower.

# Overview of Financial Statements
---
A <span style='color:#fa8231'>complete set of financial statements</span> consist of :
- Statement of comprehensive income (**Can be further spilt into**)
	- Income
	- Other Comprehensive Income
- Balance Sheet
- Statement of Changes of Equity
- Statement of Cash Flows
- Notes to financial statements comprising, summary of significant accounting policies & other explanatory information

Sometimes, certain documents are <mark style='background:#0fb9b1'>classified</mark>, which means its contents are further subdivided into :
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Current</span> - <mark style='background:#f7b731'>Less than a year</mark> to convert into cash or is due in less than a year
- <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Non-current</span> - <mark style='background:#f7b731'>More than a year</mark> to convert into cash or is due more than a year

Note that the <span style='color:#f7b731'>most liquid asset will be at the top of the list</span> this is for <span style='color:#20bf6b'>assets</span>. For <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>liabilities</span>, the <span style='color:#f7b731'>earliest due obligation</span> will be at the top.
## Balance Sheet

It <span style='color:#fa8231'>reports</span> a businesses:
1) **Assets** - <span style='color:#f7b731'>Resources</span> like land, equipment
2) **Liabilities** - <span style='color:#f7b731'>Obligations</span> like rent, loans
3) **Equity** - <span style='color:#f7b731'>Owner's Stakes</span> in the company

Some <span style='color:#fa8231'>examples</span> of **assets** are accounts receivable (Revenue yet to earned), buildings, inventory and cash.

Some <span style='color:#fa8231'>examples</span> **Liabilities**, are accounts payable (Items purchased that has not been paid), income taxes, mortgage payable, unearned revenue (Money paid but service not given), notes payable (Usually for loans as it includes interest).

Some <span style='color:#fa8231'>examples</span> **Equities** are capital stock (Money received from stocks) and retained earnings (After dividend issued), accumulated other comprehensive income (Other equity).
### Limitations of a Balance Sheet

**Assets are not at market value**
>Assets recorded are <span style='color:#f7b731'>at purchase cost not at current market value</span>, this is known as <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>depreciation</span>.

**Not everything is included**
> Not all economic assets are included in the balance sheet (Human talents, internally generated brand).

**Book value less than market value**
> A companies accounting <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>book value </span>(<span style='color:#f7b731'>amount of equity</span>) is usually less than their <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>market value</span> (<span style='color:#f7b731'>market price</span> per X number of <span style='color:#f7b731'>shares</span>).

## Statement of Comprehensive Income

It <span style='color:#fa8231'>reports</span> a businesses:
1) **Revenue**
2) **Expenses**
3) **Net Income** which is <span style='color:#20bf6b'>Revenue</span> - <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>Expenses</span>
4) **Other comprehensive income** which is <span style='color:#20bf6b'>Net Income</span> - <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Comprehensive Income</span>

This is usually recorded <span style='color:#f7b731'>over an accounting period</span> (1 Year).
### Revenue & Expenses

For wholesalers and retailers, the <span style='color:#f7b731'>largest expense is usually the cost of goods sold</span>. Gross Profit is calculated as such : $$\text{Gross Profit } = \text{Sales } - \text{Cost of Goods Sold}$$
Gains and losses also appear on the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>income statement</span> and are <span style='color:#f7b731'>expressed in net amount</span>. This refer to gains or losses on <span style='color:#f7b731'>activities outside the normal course of business</span>, e.g., selling of old equipment previously used in operations.

As mentioned, a comprehensive can be <span style='color:#fa8231'>reported into 2 parts</span>
- **Income** which is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>report until net income</span>
- **Comprehensive Income** which <span style='color:#f7b731'>starts with net income</span> until <span style='color:#f7b731'>other comprehensive income</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Earnings per share</span> is calculated as such :$$\text{Earnings per share } = \frac{\text{Net Income}}{\text{Outstanding number of shares}}$$If company<span style='color:#f7b731'> makes a loss</span> for the year, will be “Loss per share”.
## Statement of Changes in Equity

It reports <span style='color:#f7b731'>changes in the components in equity for a period of time</span>.

It is calculated as such : $$\text{Equity (Ending Bal )} = \text{Equity (Begining )} + \text{CSI} + \text{Net Income} - \text{Div} + \text{OCI}$$Where :
- **CSI** is capital stock increase
- **OCI** is other comprehensive income during the year
- All values are retrieved <mark style='background:#f7b731'>for the year only</mark>
## Statement of Retained Earnings

### Retain Earnings

It is basically <span style='color:#f7b731'>net income</span> which has been <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>retained</span> by the business. Those that are <span style='color:#f7b731'>not are distributed as dividend</span>. Some examples are : 
- Retained earning balance (Beginning)
- Net income for the year
- Dividends during the year
- Closing balance at the end of the year

It is calculated as such : $$\text{Retained earnings (End) } = \text{Retained earnings (Beginning)} +\text{Net Income } - \text{Dividends}$$There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>2 ways</span> in which <span style='color:#fa8231'>retain earning can fluctuate</span> :
1) It accumulates or <span style='color:#20bf6b'>goes up</span> if, <span style='color:#f7b731'>net income is more</span> than dividend issued
2) It <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>goes down</span>, if the company <span style='color:#f7b731'>makes a loss</span> or <span style='color:#f7b731'>more dividend is issued</span> compared to net income

## Statement of Cash Flows

It reports an <span style='color:#f7b731'>entities cash movements</span> (Inflow / Outflow) for a period of time and can be classified into <span style='color:#f7b731'>operating, investing and financing activities</span>.

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>cash balance</span> can be calculated as such : $$\text{CB (End)} = \text{CB (Start)} \pm \text{Operating} \pm \text{Investing} \pm \text{Financing Activites}$$
Where :
- **CB** stands for cash balance
- If its <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>outflow</span> it will be <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>minus</span>, if its <span style='color:#20bf6b'>inflow</span> it will be <span style='color:#20bf6b'>plus</span>
