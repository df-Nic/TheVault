# Purpose of Statement of Cash Flows
---
Cash is an important asset as it helps companies to operate. Thus this statement <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>summarises a companies cash inflow and outflow</span> for the year.

The information on how cash is generated and used is useful for investors and creditors to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>evaluate a company to generate positive net cash flows</span> and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>avoid solvency risks</span>.

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>Cash comprises of </span>: 
- Cash on hand
- Demand deposits
- Short term highly liquid investments with maturities less than 3 months
# Operating Activities
---
These activities are part of the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>companies primary operations</span> that is also their <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>primary source of revenue</span>.

Cash flow (**CF**) from operating activities is a <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>key indicator</mark></b> to determine the profitability of a company. The higher the CF the better the ability to relay debt, pay dividends, innovate new products without the need for other financing.

In the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>long run</span> companies will need a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>positive net cash flow</span> from operating activities as it determines the net income and sustainability

**Examples of operating activities**
- Sales from goods and services, this **includes accounts receivable**
- Cash payments to suppliers
- Expenses incurred like salaries and operating expenses
# Investing Activities
---
These activates are made by companies who spent cash to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>invest and generate future income and cashflows</span>. Only <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>expenditures that result in recognised asset</span> (Bonds, Stock) are classified as investing activities.

A <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>negative</span> cashflow means that they are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>investing</span> but a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>positive</span> cashflow means that they are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>selling their portfolio</span>.

**Examples of investing activities**
- Cash payments to equipment, property, plant, land, securities
- Cash advances and proceeds from the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>lending</span> (**Investing**) and collection of notes and loans from third parties.

# Financing Activities
---
These activities arise from financing activities that is useful in <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>predicting claims on future CF</span> by providers of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>financing to the entitiy</span>.

These include :
1) **Equity financiering**
	>Issuing shares to get investors to buy shares in exchange for cash
2) **Debt financing**
	>These are loans from the bank

These activates will result in <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>repayment</span> to owners, lender or creditors.

**Examples of financing activities**
- Issuing shares or other equity instruments
- Cash payments to reacquire shares from share holders
- Loans, notes, bonds, mortgages any short term or long term <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>borrowing</span>
- Repayments to any debt

# Preparing Cash Flow Statement
---
Before making the cash flow statement, start off by <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>reviewing each cash receipts and payments</span> during the period and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>group them into</span> :
- **Operating activities**
- **Investing activities**
- **Financing activities**

Some <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>non cash items</span> are, depreciation, amortisation and estimated credit loss
