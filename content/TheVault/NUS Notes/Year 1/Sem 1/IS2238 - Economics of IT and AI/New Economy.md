# What is a New Economy
---

Companies who <span style='color:#f7b731'>incoporates cutting edge technology</span> which is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>driving force of economic growth</span>.

The <span style='color:#f7b731'>transition of economic trend </span>from a manufacturing-based economy to a service-based economy. And recently from a <span style='color:#f7b731'>service-based economy to a technology and innovation-based economy</span>.

Examples of companies which are part of the new economy:
1) TikTok
2) Youtube
3) Google
4) Amazon

Companies now are figuring out how to incorporate technology into their business model which will lead to a better product or service to gain a competitive edge.

**Goldilocks' Economy**
> It describes an ideal state for an economic system where there is full employment, economic stability and stable growth. There is no large margin of expansion and contraction

**Ideal conditions for a perfect economy:**
1)  Low unemployment
2)  Asset price inflation
3)  Low market interest rates
4)  Low inflation
5)  Steady gross domestic product (GDP)
	If the GDP is too low it suffers and <span style='color:#f7b731'>economic downturn</span> (recession), too fast it will lead to an <span style='color:#f7b731'>inflation</span>

## Effects of the New Economy

1) Speed

With the <span style='color:#f7b731'>integration</span> of technology, companies are able to churn out <span style='color:#f7b731'>more products quickly</span> and also with a <span style='color:#f7b731'>consistent quality</span>.

2) More employment opportunities in the tech sector

With the rise of technology based companies, the demand for tech skilled individuals are steadily increasing.

3) Increased Productivity

Technology as helped in <span style='color:#f7b731'>streamlining tedious business processes</span> which can be automated to maximize their manufacturing capabilities of goods and services. 

4) Customer Driven business

With the new economy, its structure is based on <span style='color:#f7b731'>customer demand</span>. With this leads to <span style='color:#f7b731'>increase business competition</span>, <span style='color:#f7b731'>new innovative ideas</span> and improved <span style='color:#f7b731'>access to information</span>.

## What is Productivity

It is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>measure of economic or business performance</span> that indicates how efficiently people, companies, industries, and whole economies convert inputs, such as labor and capital, into outputs, such as goods or services.

When productivity is high, it means that a companies <span style='color:#f7b731'>business process is efficient</span>. With technology being a key factor for increased productivity.

This however is <span style='color:#f7b731'>not the case for all business</span>. Take for example Hermes a luxury brand which people value its products due to its scarcity and high quality making it desirable. If they were to increase productivity;
1) There will be <mark class="hltr-orange">more supply</mark> of Hermes Products
2) <mark class="hltr-orange">Prices will drop</mark> as not everyone can afford it and since there is an increase in supply, it will drop such that the company can earn a profit
3) Which does not reflect on their business model of offering high end, luxury and rare products.

## Moore's Law

It states that number of transistors on a microchip doubles every two years. Thus it is expected that the <span style='color:#f7b731'>speed and capability of computers will increase</span> every 2 years which results <span style='color:#f7b731'>in less cost</span>.

However in 2010, the advancement is beginning to slow down (<span style='color:#f7b731'>Limit of cost effectiveness</span>). The cost reduction to efficiency is dwindling or is no longer feasible.

## Hype Cycle

It representing the <span style='color:#f7b731'>maturity, adoption and social application</span> of specific technologies

**It helps in**:
- Separation of hype from the real drivers of a technology’s commercial promise  
- Reduce the risk of your technology investment decisions  
- Compare your understanding of a technology’s business value with the objectivity of experienced IT analysts

1) Innovation Trigger
	An potential technology breakthrough, with some concept which companies might be interested to try and integrate with their business

2) Peak of Inflated Expectations
	Companies will find ways to succeed with using technology, which others companies might use their idea.

3) Trough of Disillusionment
	Through time, these technologies might get less interested as implementations fails to deliver. Only those that benefit might continue to invest in it.

4) Slope of Enlightenment
	More detailed analysis on how that specific technology can benefit companies and variations of it will emerge.

5) Plateau of Productivity
	Mainstream adoption of that technology will take off and majority of the market will be applying it in their business to gain its benefits.
