# Narrative
---
They are just **stories** we tell to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>help understand the world</span> or <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>how we can process new information</span>.

It involves the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>omission of "unimportant" information</span>, we just want to **get the point across**.

However, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>narrative are not natural</span>, it is **created by someone** and they <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>decide what is important</span> and what is not.

> [!note] What is History?
> It can be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>viewed</span> as:
> - Actual past events
> - Narrative of past events
> - Scholarly study using surviving evidence

With **narratives**, it <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>shapes who people are</span> (*how they act*), but <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-red)'>not all narratives are equally true</mark></b> (*not neutral*).

> [!info] Public Memory
> From **individual memory of the past**, it is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>shared to the public</span> in some way or form.
> 
> Some examples are:
> - Museums
> - Monuments
> - Celebrations & ceremonies
>   
>   Sometimes it is good to look at when the monument is build as well as the history it is talking about.
