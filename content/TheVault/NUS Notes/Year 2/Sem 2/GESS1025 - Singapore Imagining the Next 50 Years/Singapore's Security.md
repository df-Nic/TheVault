# Singapore's Security
---
<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Singapore's geography</span></b> plays a **major aspect in its security**.

Singapore location make it a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>strategic location for trade</span></b>. It also has a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>small land size</span></b> with a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>lack of natural resources</span></b>.

Thus Singapore **security is at a disadvantage** by its small size and its reliance of natural resources from others. It has to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>remain vigilant</span></b>.

With the small land and **lack of manpower**, its <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>military is not strong</span></b> as compared to others. This also leads to very <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>little strategic depth</span></b>, meaning it <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>cannot cede space to an attacker and taking it back after regrouping</span></b>.

<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Security is the protection of acquired values</span></b>, and some of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>Singapore values</span> are:
- Sovereignty 
- Territorial integrity (*land, sea and air space*)
- Social cohesion

> [!note] There is a correlation between economy & security
> To strengthen the military Singapore will need to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>purchase better equipment if they can afford it</span></b>.
> 
> In additional for economic stability, it heavily relies on the effectiveness of its <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>defence force as it attracts more foreign investments</span></b>.
# Regional Dynamics
---
First we need to understand how Singapore's national interests and strategic cultures came to be. During the cold war it was the fight between communist and non communist parties. Countries that were decolonising became battlefields.

Singapore during that time **chose to be non-aligned**, meaning it did <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>not commit to taking sides</span></b> but will <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>engage with both sides for mutual benefits and where interest aligned</span></b>.

> [!info] Domino theory
> The US fears that during the cold war if south Vietnam falls to communism the rest will follow suit, which Singapore agrees.

However later on **British redraw troops** from Singapore and Malaysia, <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>requiring Singapore to have its own military</span></b> (*conscription act in 1967*) as it cannot rely on British forces.

ASEAN formation in 1967 helped in the stability and peace in south east Asia. It also ensure that Vietnam did not expand its sphere of influence of communism.

Singapore stand with Cambodia who was occupied by Vietnam and helped to keep its seat in the UN.
# Rise of Unconventional Threats
---
The <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>new world order</span></b> coined was to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>protect small states from aggression</span></b>, which was good for Singapore. It also facilitated economic development.

Singapore should <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>remain adaptable and relevant to bigger power to stay afloat</span></b> (*good relations with China & India*).

> [!info] Human security
> Aside from national security, human security is to focus on <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>protecting individuals</span></b>.

The **source of the risk** is not usually the state but rather <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>groups of people or the environment</span></b>.

Singapore involvement with the US in the invasion in Iraq was to maintain good relations with the US but also the actions taken will need to maintain social cohesion in Singapore.

Terrorism can happen to any country and Singapore has to be prepared for it

Through it may happen, lives are lost, buildings destroyed, trust diminished. But all these can be built back over time. Singapore needs to be resilient.

However the <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>social fabric is very hard to mend</span></b>.

> [!question] How can Singapore maintain racial harmony?
> It is difficult to track everyone leaving Singapore to join these groups. A better way is to raise awareness to these groups in Singapore making them be more alert, while asking the rest to not blame one another

<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Non-traditional security threats</span></b> usually affects the individuals

- Pollution

- Cyber attacks

**Environment disasters** in the region can affect Singapore.

**Financial crisis** translate to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>political instability</span></b> which affects Singapore's diplomatic relations.

Singapore reliance of **global connectivity** also <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>linked to its economic well-being</span></b>, major hubs like Singapore will not function properly if there are pandemics.
# Managing Threats
---
Singapore build a strong military through the **national service system** to defend itself. It is seen as to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>foster national unity & communal harmony</span></b>.

The **army** also made Singapore a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>key stakeholder in international collective efforts</span></b>.

> [!info] Force multiplier
> Any capability when added to and employed by the defence force <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>significantly increases the combat potential</span></b>.

Singapore **spare no expense on developing a strong military**. But it sends a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>strong signal to other countries</span></b>.  It <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>relies on skills, knowledge, information and technology</span></b> for up and coming new threats.

Singapore works hard to **build a strong and economic defence and diplomatic relations** with other countries. All this is to ensure that its <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>neighbours & major powers have vested interest in Singapore's wellbeing</span></b>.

Thus Singapore has been highly active in developing trade, diplomatic & security linkages with many countries.

Singapore<span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'> contributed to regional stability</span> by making common cause with neighbours through ASEAN.
- It helps to **reduce the development gap**
- **Handle disputes**

It is not only the army but in Singapore <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>everyone should play a part in its defence</span></b> (*other forms of security like economic & social*).
## Importance of ASEAN & Southeast Asia

**Singapore's neighbours plays an important role in its defence**, as they are bigger countries surrounded by ocean. Therefore <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Singapore builds on its geographical advantage</span> </b> since many trade routes cross Singapore.

With **ASEAN**, it makes Singapore **look like a bigger country** and thus it will be <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>easier to communicate globally</span></b>.

ASIAN developed something called <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>dialog partnership</span></b>. This includes US, Japan, China, Canada etc. This allows us to corporate with the bigger countries (*making us known to other counties*).
### Singapore Contribution to ASEAN

ASEAN started out with 5 non-communist countries in 1967, during the cold war. During this time Singapore has **contribute ideas and make good suggestions** to help the group to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>remain stable to conduct diplomatic activities</span></b>.

After the cold war is over, Singapore wanted to **move on to globalisation, economic development and opening up of trade markets**. It rally the other ASEAN countries to move quickly on globalisation initiatives.

Singapore <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>proactiveness provided reassurance to the balance of power</span></b> in southeast Asia.
