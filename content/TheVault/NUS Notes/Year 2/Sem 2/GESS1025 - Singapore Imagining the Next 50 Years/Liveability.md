# About Liveable Cities
---
From the UN-Habitat, in the 1950's 1/3 of the world's population is living in cities, in 2000 1/2 and by 2050 its projected to be 2/3.

> [!info] Mega cities
> They are essentially cities which contain <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>more than 10,000,000 people</span></b>.

However, besides population we need to also look at **density**, which is the <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>number of individuals per unit area or volume</span></b>.

The **more dense** the city has a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>negative impact in livability</span></b> (*overcrowding, poverty, crime, pollution, dieses, high cost of living*). A congested city with low quality urban services, increase competition of people use these facilities.

Highley built up cities are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>shaped and challenged by a number of factors</span>:
1) Shifts in population and demography
2) Transformation to housing needs
3) Changing energy needs
4) Transformation in technology
5) Understanding the environment

To <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>deal with increasing population</span> cities must make sure
- People have jobs
- Have access to amenities
- Remain liveable

<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>People & land</span></b> are the 2 key aspects to build liveable cities.

There are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>2 pivots</span> to achieve a liveable city:
1) **Planning** and **development** which are <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>integrated & long-term</span></b>
2) **Governance** which are <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>visionary & pragmatic</span></b> (*make decisions which are beneficial not popular*)

> [!abstract] Planning long-term
> It is about <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>looking ahead</span></b> to foresee problems and solve them or to decide on tradeoffs for better productivity.
> 
> It is also about <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>flexibility</span></b> allowing change when needed.

Cities will also need to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>innovate systematically</span></b>, instead of leaving it to chance, but leaning from others and applying it to their own context.

<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Dynamic urban governance</span></b>, which are leader to interact with citizens and stakeholders to strike a balance with competing demands.

Government must also have **strong integrity** to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>prevent corruption</span></b>.

It also understands the need of <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>involving the community as stakeholders</span></b>.

Smart cities to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>work with markets to harness efficiencies</span></b>, balancing private sector involvement.
# How Does Singapore Maintain Liveability
---
Some statistics of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>Singapore's land and population</span>:
- 710 square kilometers
	- North to south: 33.9 kilometers
	- East to west: 53.32 kilometers
- Population: 5.703 billion as of 2019
- Density: 8147 people per square kilometer
- But the total building space is about 161.4 square kilometers
- Thus only <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>27% of Singapore's land is for housing thus high density</span></b>

But despite its high density, **Singapore** is ranked as a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>highly liveable city</span></b>. How is this so?

> [!question] What does Singapore offer to make itself liveable?
> 1) Competitive economy
> 2) High quality of life
> 3) Sustainable environment

When **planning on Singapore's land usage**, an <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>holistic, forward-looking and integrated planning framework</span></b> is used.

> [!summary] How urban redevelopment authority plans Singapore's usage
> - **Concept plan**: Guidelines of development and safeguards lands for future needs
> - **Master plan**: Translate concept plan strategies to an actual plan for development

Firstly, Singapore has <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>good governance</span></b> which led to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>strong development of infrastructure</span></b>.

One key aspect of Singapore that it is **walkable**, everything is <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>connected</span></b> (*Less focus on vehicles*).

Singapore also focus on itself bring a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>clean and green city</span></b>. **Lining up the building in a neat way** also displays some sort of organisation and cleanliness.

**How buildings are placed and built is also important**, it can make Singapore <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>feel larger and more spacious and free</span></b>. For example social housing.
# Challenges of a Liveable City
---
## Climate Change

To make a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>big impact the bigger cities must take charge</span></b>. But even though Singapore is small the government has done a lot to control its effect on global warming.

> [!example] Singapore's cars are checked every 3 to 5 years to make sure there is no undue pollution in the exhaust

Singapore built a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>efficient transportation system</span></b> (*MRT & bus network*) to reduce the burning of fossil fuels.

> [!info] Development should not only think about the people but the environment as well

**Nature plays a crucial role** in everything:
- Sustainability
- Temperatures (<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>urban heat island effect</span></b>)

Singapore is now focusing on <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>giving more land for green areas</span></b> and <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>increasing green replacement</span></b> in the build environment.

> [!quote] Wellbeing of people with nature
> Nature can survive without humans, but humans cannot survive without nature.
## Aging Population

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>key concerns in an aging population</span> are:
- Social economic (*declining birthrate*)
- Healthcare (*Increase healthcare needs & cost or keep elderly healthy*)

One thing Singapore did is to provide <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>incentives for families buying housing near their parents</span></b>.

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>Key aspects to aging</span> (*community ageing with place*):
1) Community (*Social aspect*)
2) Aging (*Bio physical and medical aspect*)
3) Place (*Environment aspect*)

> [!info] Community
> The community must have a shared region and work together. They have to define what kind of community they want to be and how they provide care for the elderly (*or for everyone*).

A challenge Singapore face if they want to take ideas from other countries is that <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>most of the resident population in public housing</span></b> (*80%*), which <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>means high density and high rise</span></b>.

> [!example] An example of what Siingapore has done is Kampung Admiralty
> It is a building with elderly homes, medical facilities and community amenities (*community plaza*).
### Lessons from Japan

Japan has a **diverse** choices for caring for the elderly. They obviously have the long term options like nursing homes.

They also have something called <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>assisted living and group homes</span></b> (*small groups of elderly taken care by the community*).
## Challenges in the Next 50 Years

For sustainability, the end goal is for <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Singapore to be self-sufficient</span></b> (*energy, food, water*).

Singapore should also aim to have <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>100% green replacement</span></b>, to reduce a cities carbon footprint, recycle for resources without having to extract it from earth to achieve a <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>circular economy</span></b>.

<b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Maintain the human to human relationship in communities</span></b>. This all depends on the design of the neighbourhood and development of the community.
