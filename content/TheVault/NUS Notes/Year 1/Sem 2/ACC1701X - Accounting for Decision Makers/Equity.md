# Financing Activities
---
Every business will need to raise financing to start the operations. The business entity can <span style='color:#f7b731'>borrow</span> (debt) or <span style='color:#f7b731'>issue share capital</span> to raise money.

Using debt carries a <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>higher risk of solvency</span> as it is a fixed obligation to repay a specified amount, on a fixed date. However using equity through <span style='color:#f7b731'>issuance of share capital</span>, is the most common way of financing. All the <span style='color:#f7b731'>risks</span> and rewards from investment in the business rest <span style='color:#f7b731'>on the investors or owners</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Loan</span>
>It is a fixed, legal <span style='color:#f7b731'>obligation</span> to repay a specified amount with interest, whether the borrower performs poorly or well

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Investment</span>
>A higher risk of losing money, balanced by the chance of <span style='color:#f7b731'>sharing in the wealth creation if the company does well</span>

# Issuing of Shares
---
**Corporation**
>A <span style='color:#f7b731'>legal entity</span> created by shareholders, or stockholders, with the purpose of operating for profit. Corporation being an entity, can enter into contracts, sue and be sued, own assets, and borrow money.

**Characteristics of a corporation**
- Limited liability
- Easy to transfer ownership
- Ability to raise large amounts of capital
- Subjected to government rules and regulations including taxation

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Par value</span>
>It is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>legal capital</span> of entity that <span style='color:#f7b731'>cannot be returned</span> to the investors as long as the corporation still exists. It is intended to protect creditors

A nominal value usually $1 has <span style='color:#f7b731'>no corelation</span> to the market share value and companies can issues share without par value.

If the par-value share (**Price of the share**) is issued <span style='color:#f7b731'>above par value</span>, then it is said it is issued at a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>premium</span>. So when <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>ordinary shares</span> are issued at premium, the <span style='color:#f7b731'>difference</span> between <span style='color:#f7b731'>par value and issued price</span> is <span style='color:#f7b731'>credited</span> into the account :
- Paid-in capital in Excess of Par, Ordinary Shares

**Example**
- Let a company issue 1000 shares ordinary, par value is $1 and 1 share is $50
- **Debit** cash ($1000 \times \text{Price per share}$)
- **Credit** ordinary shares $1000 \times \text{Par value}$ and Paid-in capital in Excess of Par, Ordinary Shares ($1000 \times (50 - 1)$)
> If there is <span style='color:#fa8231'>no par value</span>, then there will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>no paid-in capital in excess</span>

If <span style='color:#fa8231'>cash was not used</span> but exchanged for some asset :
- The assets are <span style='color:#f7b731'>recorded in fair value</span> (The price of the asset)
- If it cannot be determined, then the <span style='color:#f7b731'>fair market value of the shares should be used</span>
- Recording is the same as above, but the cash account will change


There is also <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>preference shares</span> which is totally different from ordinary shares and they have different accounts as well, just <span style='color:#f7b731'>change the ordinary shares to preference shares</span>.

# Repurchasing Shares
---
This happens when a company <span style='color:#f7b731'>buys back its own shares in the share market</span>, these repurchased shares are called <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>treasure shares</span> and they are a <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>contra-equity account</mark></b>.

When buying back, <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>par value is disregarded</mark></b>.

<span style='color:#fa8231'>Reasons</span> to buy back shares :
- Remove outstanding shares to prevent a hostile takeover
- It increases earnings per share which often increases share price (Less quantity, Higher demand possible)
- Entice trading in the company
- Reissue treasure shares to employees as bonus or a plan

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>buys back shares</span> :
- **Debit** treasury shares ($\text{Amount} \times \text{{Price per share}}$)
- **Credit** Cash

It is also possible for companies to <span style='color:#fa8231'>reissue treasury shares</span> :
- **Credit** cash ($\text{Amount} \times \text{{Price per share}}$)
- **Debit** treasury shares & **paid-in capital, treasury shares**

Note that the amount for **treasury shares** uses the <span style='color:#f7b731'>price when the company brought back</span>, while **paid in capital** is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>difference of the prices,  credit for positive, negative is debit</span>.

If **paid-in capital, treasury shares** <span style='color:#fa8231'>goes below negative debit balance</span>, the balance will <span style='color:#f7b731'>debit to retain earnings</span>.
# Retained Earnings & Dividends
---
**Retained Earnings**
>Portion of the companies equity that is <span style='color:#f7b731'>earned from profitable operations</span> and <span style='color:#f7b731'>not distributed to shareholders</span>

**Dividend**
>Is the distribution of profits to shareholders on a pro-rata basis which needs the approval of the shareholders. This <span style='color:#f7b731'>decreases the retained earnings</span>

Dividends can be <span style='color:#fa8231'>issued</span> in the form of :
1) **Cash Dividend**
2) **Share Dividend**

People holding <span style='color:#fa8231'>treasury shares</span>, it <span style='color:#f7b731'>will not receive any dividend</span> no matter what since it is like the company paying itself.

When the company <span style='color:#f7b731'>declares</span> that they are going to <span style='color:#f7b731'>issue dividends</span> is called the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>declaration date</span> :
- **Debit** cash dividends (Spilt into the types of shares and their respective amounts)
- **Credit** cash dividends payable (Paying in the future)

At **year end**, <span style='color:#f7b731'>dividends account is closed to retained earnings</span> (Decrease), this is the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>closing entry</span> :
- **Debit** retained earnings
- **Credit** cash dividends

The day that the <span style='color:#f7b731'>company actually pays dividends</span> is called the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>dividend payment date</span> :
- **Debit** cash dividends payable
- **Credit** cash

If the company just <span style='color:#fa8231'>establishes all the shareholders</span> who are entitled to the dividend then <span style='color:#f7b731'>no journal entry is needed</span>.
## Dividend for Preference Shares

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>2 types of preference shares</span> :
1) **Non-cumulative** preference shares

A preference share receives a <span style='color:#f7b731'>dividend percentage</span> on the <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>preference shares par value</mark></b>. Preferred share holders has the <span style='color:#f7b731'>right to dividend</span> first before ordinary share holders.

There can be a <span style='color:#f7b731'>cap on the amount given</span> and if it exceeds then it will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>upper bounded</span> by the cap.

However their <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>amount is capped</span> unlike ordinary share holders due to the dividend percentage.

2) **Cumulative** preference shares

Similar to non-cumulative however, any <span style='color:#f7b731'>outstanding amount</span> that is due in previous years will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>carried over</span>.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Dividends in Arrears</span>
>Missed dividends in past years will be paid in the current year if dividends are declared. It does <span style='color:#f7b731'>not represent actual liabilities</span> and are reported in the notes to the financial statements

Now preferred shareholders will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>paid dividends in arrears and current dividends before</span> ordinary share holders.

## Share Dividends

So far everything above has been using cash dividends, for <span style='color:#f7b731'>small share dividends</span> **(20 - 25%)**, they are <span style='color:#f7b731'>assigned at market value</span>. While <span style='color:#f7b731'>large share dividends are assigned at par value</span>.

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>declaring share dividends</span> :
- **Debit** share dividends ($\text{Market value/Par value} \times \text{Amount}$)
- **Credit** share dividends distributable (Is just a payable account)
- **Credit** Paid-in capital in excess of par ($\text{Difference} \times \text{Amount}$)
# Share Spilt
---
A share spilt increases the number of <span style='color:#f7b731'>outstanding shares</span> and also the <span style='color:#f7b731'>par or stated value per share also decreases</span>. This just allows increase accessibility to investors as the price decreases.

For a **2 by 1 spilt**, the <span style='color:#20bf6b'>outstanding shares doubled</span> while the <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>share value is halved</span>.

|                  Items Affected                   | Stock Spilt |
| :-----------------------------------------------: | :---------: |
|           Number of shares outstanding            |  Increase   |
|                   Total equity                    |  No change  |
|                 Retained earnings                 |  No change  |
|                  Ordinary Shares                  |  No change  |
| Paid-in capital in excess of par, Ordinary Shares |  No change  |
|                Par Value per share                |  Decrease   |
# Equity Items In Other Comprehensive Income
---
Comprehensive income is equals to **Net Income + Other comprehensive income**.

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>SCI includes</span> :
- Revenue
- Expenses
- Gains & Losses
- Net Income
- Other Comprehensive Income

Comprehensive income can be <span style='color:#fa8231'>spilt into 2 statements</span> :
1) **Income statement**
2) **Statement of compressive income** (Start with net income)

There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>2 main other compressive income items</span> :
1) **Exchange differences arising on translation of the equity of foreign subsidiaries** which are the <span style='color:#f7b731'>losses and gains from foreign exchanges</span>
2) **Unrealised gains or losses of FVTOCI** are losses or gains financial assets due to <span style='color:#f7b731'>changes in their market values</span> (Investments)

**FVTOCI**
>It stands for Fair Value through Other Comprehensive Income

**Other comprehensive income** is <span style='color:#f7b731'>reported</span> in the balance sheet in equity <span style='color:#f7b731'>under other equity or other reserves</span>. It is the accumulated amounted of other comprehensive income.
