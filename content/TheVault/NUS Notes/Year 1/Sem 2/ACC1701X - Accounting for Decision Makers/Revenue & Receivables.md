# Revenue Recognition
---
There are <span style='color:#fa8231'>5 steps to identify revenue</span> :

1) **Identify contract with customer**
A contract is recognised if the following is met:
- <span style='color:#f7b731'>All parties</span> must <span style='color:#f7b731'>approve</span> of the contract and must <span style='color:#f7b731'>carry out their obligations</span>
- The entity (**seller**) can identify each parties rights regarding the goods or services to be transferred.
- The entity can<span style='color:#f7b731'> identify payment terms </span>
- The contract has <span style='color:#f7b731'>commercial substance</span> (Risk, timing, change in future cash flows)
- It is <span style='color:#f7b731'>probable</span> that the entity will <span style='color:#f7b731'>collect</span> the conidiation to which it will be entitled in exchange for goods and services to be transferred to the customer

2) **Identify performance obligations in the contract**
The seller has to <span style='color:#f7b731'>identify the performance obligations</span> in the contract.

3) **Determine the transaction price**
Based on the contract the seller must know <span style='color:#f7b731'>how much to collect</span> from the buyer

4) **Allocate the transaction price to the separate performance obligations**
Sellers should <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>pro-rate</span> the transaction price based on the different stand-alone prices for each separate performance obligation.

**Example**
Let $P_{0} = 10$ be obligation 1 and $P_{1} = 5$ be obligation 2, Then the total contract will be $\$12$ where :
- $Pr_{0} = 10 / 15 * 10$
- $Pr_{1} = 5 / 15 * 12$ 

5) **Determine when the performance obligation is satisfied and revenue can be recognized**
Transfer of <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>control</span> to the customer as <span style='color:#f7b731'>revenue recognition criteria</span>. A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>control</span> is defined as the ability to <span style='color:#f7b731'>direct the use of the assets and obtain sustainability of all their benefits</span> (Title, physical possession).

# Revenue
---
**Accounts Receivable** - It is considered as an <span style='color:#20bf6b'>asset</span> since it represents <span style='color:#f7b731'>money due</span> for services done or goods sold on credit. This only happens when <span style='color:#f7b731'>revenue is recognised and the customer pays in the future</span>.

**Contra Revenue**, it is an account that <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>deducts the gross sales</span> to obtain the <span style='color:#f7b731'>net sales</span> to be reported in the <span style='color:#f7b731'>income statement</span>.

**Entry :** 
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit accounts receivable</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit sales revenue</mark></b>, if the customer is paying in the future
- Once cash is collected <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit accounts receivable</mark></b>
- If cash is paid at the start then just <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit sales revenue</mark></b>

**Sales discounts** - It is used to <span style='color:#f7b731'>incentivise early payment</span> to <span style='color:#20bf6b'>minimise risk</span>

In <span style='color:#f7b731'>credit terms</span> it will be in the form of $2 / 10, n / 30$, which means that, if the <span style='color:#f7b731'>customer pays within 10 days</span> (<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>discount period</span>), then they will get a <span style='color:#f7b731'>2% discount</span>, else they have <span style='color:#f7b731'>30 days to pay the full amount</span>.

Assuming the credit term above and a **payment of $10000** is made.

**Company Entry :**
- If the payment is due then just <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit account receivable</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit sales discount</mark></b>

A <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>sales discount</span> is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra revenue</span>

**Customer Entry :**
- If the payment is due then just <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit account payable</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit purchase discount</mark></b>

If its purchase of goods instead of crediting purchase discount, <span style='color:#f7b731'>credit inventory</span>.

**Returns & Allowances** - Goods that are damaged can be returned for a refund, these are <span style='color:#f7b731'>recorded under sales returns and allowances</span>

**Entry :**
- Just <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit Sales Returns and Allowances</mark></b> for the amount that is returned, then if payment is made then, <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>debit cash</mark></b> and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit accounts receivable</mark></b>
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit inventory</mark></b> since the inventory is taken back and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit cost of goods sold</mark></b>.

The <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>sales returns and allowances</span> are a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra revenue</span>
# Bad Debts
---
<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Bad debts</span> are essentially <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>uncollectible</span> accounts receivable and are <span style='color:#f7b731'>recorded as an expense</span> since the company has to bear the cost.

**Expected Credit Loss** - It is considered as an <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>expense</span>, which is an <span style='color:#f7b731'>estimated expense for uncollectable amounts</span>.

**Loss Allowance** - It is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra account to accounts receivable</span> on the balance sheet. It is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>impairment</span> of accounts receivable that is estimated to be uncollectable.

**Net Realizable Value** - <span style='color:#f7b731'>Accounts receivable</span> reported on the balance sheet <span style='color:#f7b731'>minus the loss allowance</span>.

**Entry :**
Lets say a company **estimate** that of the $50000 they will **lose 10%, which is $5000** for all customers
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit expected credit loss</mark></b> for 5000 and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit loss allowance</mark></b> of 5000.

If there is <span style='color:#f7b731'>any existing balance</span> in loss allowance, after a new year just top up till $5000 or up till the adjusted amount.

If a receivable is **uncollectable**, lets say **$1000**, then
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit loss allowance</mark></b> by 1000 and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit accounts receivable</mark></b>.

If the customer pays back but is written off then just <span style='color:#f7b731'>reverse the above entry</span>.

There is another way to estimate loss, which is through the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>aging accounts receivable</span>. Each receivable is <span style='color:#f7b731'>categorised by the number of days it has been outstanding</span>.

Each category is multiplied by some percentage, the <span style='color:#f7b731'>older the higher the percentage</span>.

# Notes Receivable
---
In short it is accounts receivable but with <span style='color:#f7b731'>interest</span>. It is a form of a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>promissory note</span> with the following :
- **Face value**
- **Issuance Date**
- **Due date or maturity**
- **Interest Rate**

The interest revenue follows the <span style='color:#f7b731'>simple interest</span> formula :
$$
\text{Interest revenue } = P \times r \times t
$$
**Where :**
- $P$ is the face amount
- $r$ is the annual interest rate
- $t$ is the term of note (Duration)

**Entry :**
Lets say a company agree to **open a notes receivable account for the remaining $5000**, with a interest of 12%
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit notes receivable</mark></b> by 5000 and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit accounts receivable</mark></b>.

In 3 months time, the interest to be paid is $5000 * 12\% * 3 = 150$, thus when the company gets paid
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit cash</mark></b> by 5150 and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit notes receivable</mark></b> of 5000 and <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit interest revenue</mark></b> of 150.

# Foreign Currency
---
Transactions in other currencies are receptible to price <span style='color:#f7b731'>fluctuation</span>, companies will keep track of the foreign exchange loss or gain. 

This will be reflected under the account <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>foreign exchange gain or loss</span>. where the original price is determined at the date of sale.

To <span style='color:#20bf6b'>reduce risk</span>,
- Make the transaction in terms of the primary currency, let the customer take the risk
- Enter a forward contract to hedge the exchange risk

# Receivables Management
---
The <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>accounts receivable turnover</span> is a measure on how <span style='color:#f7b731'>fast a company collects its receivables</span>. The <span style='color:#f7b731'>higher the better</span>.

Average net AR = (Beginning AR + Ending AR)/ 2

$$
\text{Accounts receivable turnover } = \frac{\text{Net sales}}{\text{Average net accounts receivable}}
$$

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Average collection period</span>, this turnover can be converted into the <span style='color:#f7b731'>average</span> number of days to collect receivables.
$$
\text{Average collection period } = \frac{365}{\text{Accounts receivable turnover}}
$$

The <span style='color:#f7b731'>lower</span> the average the <span style='color:#f7b731'>more efficient</span> the collection is. With a <span style='color:#f7b731'>higher average</span>, then the company might be <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>cash poor</span>, as majority of the cash is in receivables and they might be having <span style='color:#f7b731'>short-term loans</span> to cover the cash shortage or <span style='color:#f7b731'>losing interest as the cash can be invested</span>.
