# Language & Dialect
---
The **nation** <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>defines the national language</span>. In the past people believe that to have a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>nation is the only way to progress</span>.

> [!quote] From Benedict Anderson
> The nation is an imagined political community

Thus a **nation is another social construct** but it does <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>not mean it is fragile or unimportant</span>.

Sometimes the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>land the country resides in define the nation</span>.
## Dialect & Nationalism in China

China feel that having **one unified national language** to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>unify the country</span> while others feel that **dialects is a linguistic diversity** and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>does not stand in the way of unity</span>.

There are some <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>shared assumptions</span> of the language debate"
- The **importance of the Han identity** where <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>people will get included or excluded</span>
- **Believe in science** and its <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>importance to progress</span>
- **Respect for authenticity**, the sense of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>nostalgia and fear of losing Chinese traditions</span>

Today there is a **decline of many fangyan**, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>variety of factors</span>:
- Education policy and focus on Putonghua in schools
- Domestic migration to cities, relative dominance of Putonghua in urban areas
- Social media and the homogenization of online culture
- Preservation not seen as a pressing need
## National Language for Indonesia

Their **national language is Bahasa Indonesia** however most <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Indonesians speak it as a second language</span>.

> [!question] Why is Javanese not the national language?
> Java makes up majority of Indonesia but is not the national language
> 1) Really difficult to learn!
> 2) Many registers based on social status, contains many honorifics and humilifics
> 	- Contributes to Point 1
> 	- Some nationalists argue it is “anti-democratic”
> 3) Javanese by far the largest ethnic group, but not the majority
