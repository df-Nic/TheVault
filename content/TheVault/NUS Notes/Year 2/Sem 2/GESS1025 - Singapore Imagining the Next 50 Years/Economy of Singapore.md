# Macroeconomics of Singapore
---
Singapore has a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>outstanding trade performance</span> in 2008 its trade GDP is the best in the world. Despite its <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>limited natural resources, land & manpower</span> it has achieve a **high GDP in relative to its size**.

> [!question] How is Singapore's GDP now?
> Currently it <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>has been on the decline</span> however this is a <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>natural phenomenon as economies mature</mark></b>.

## Strategies Singapore Employed

Singapore's economy grow is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>due to government policies and environmental factors</span>.

> [!question] What did Singapore do to achieve economic success?
> It is **important to know whether this growth model is sustainable**
> - Multinational & <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>foreign investment</span> centered model
> - Singapore was a city state and they <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>focus as a regional and global hub</span> (*marine and air ports*)
>   
> However <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>this growth model is not significant any more</span> because, Singapore can no longer import technology from others and it needs to rely on innovation and domestic initiative.
### Foreign Investment

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Foreign direct instruments</span> (*FDI*) was important to Singapore's growth. How it helped was not because of the money but <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>more on the package</span> of:
- Management
- Technology
- Branding
- Distribution
- Business process 

At **independence** Singapore <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>turned to foreign investments</span>. Incentives attracted investment which leads to companies setting up operations and then create jobs for Singaporeans.

There is a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>movement from manufacturing to services</span> through FDI as they **move away from lover value activities**
### Exports

Singapore has also embarked on an <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>active export promotion strategy</span> to **attract more foreign investments**. Move away from lower value products to higher value exports to maintain export competitiveness.

> [!note] Significant of exports in Singapore
> Firstly <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>Singapore has scarce resources</span>, thus it needs to Invite foreign investments to set up factories to create jobs
> 
> Singapore then need to **go global in the trade strategy** thus the reason for <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>attracting other investments who produced for the global market</span>.

One key player to help Singapore to achieve this and go global is **INTRACO** also known as<span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'> international trading company</span>:
- It <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>provided Singapore an outlet</span> for its products to the global market
- <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Brought materials in bulk</span> for manufacturing and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>reducing raw material costs</span>
- Helped <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>access communist economies</span> without involving political aspects
## Labour Force

Singapore faces <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>issues with man power</span> due to its **small population**. In addition due to this there has been an <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>increase in demand for non-resident manpower</span>.

> [!info] Resident & non-resident manpower
> **Resident manpower** as the name suggests are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>local workers</span>.
> 
> While **non-resident manpower** are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>foreign workers</span>,

With Singapore aging population the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>age distribution of the labour forces has been shifting</span>. With a small population, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>reliance of foreign manpower has increased</span>.

There are some tends within the resident workers, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>male participation has been on a downward trend while female participation has been increasing</span>,

However with policies for older workers such as the **special employment credit** has allowed Singapore to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>maintain its labour market participation</span>, however it is still low.

With access to higher education more people in the workforce have a degree. This also caused the shift in the importance to the service sector before independence then after independent it decreases but it increased again later on.

**Rate of growth of employment salary** has been <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>declining</span>, however there are some <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>policies to assist</span> this such as the:
- **Progressive wage model** to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>improve productivity & skills</span> thus increasing income.
- **Workfare scheme**, to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>supplement income of low wage workers</span> to help them find work and stay in work. It was inspired by the negative income tax in the US which supplements the income and not take it away. To achieve this CPF contributions will be reduced and government will provide income supplements

### Tripartite Approach to Labour Market Relations

Singapore's <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>unemployment rate has worsened over the years</span>.  The goal of this is to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ensure maximum employment</span> for the citizens and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>address workers needs</span>.

> [!info] Tripartism
> It is the partnership of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>3 social partners</span>
> 1) **Government**
> 2) **Employers**
> 3) **Labor union**

This has helped Singapore's economy by <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>increasing it with equity</span>. It also <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>minimise industrial disputes & promote good symbiotic relationship</span> among the 3 parties
### Outlook for the Labour Market in Singapore

Currently the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>foreign workforce growth has slowed</span>, companies have transformed but many has resorted to hiring local workers instead of restructuring or economising on manpower.

This <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>increased the labor forced participation rate</span>, more women and elderly workers.

However this increase can cause companies to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>avoid restructuring thus local hiring will not last</span> and slow down in the coming years.
## Labour Cost & Productivity

Having a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>better productivity</span> will mean a:
- **Better standard of living**
- **Increase in income**
- **Cost competitiveness** due to the increase in income

Singapore to have a **vibrant economy** and be **competitive in technological frontier**, they will need to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>continue to be innovative</span> in exploring new forms of production and creating new markets.

Reliant of trade, cause cost competitiveness to be a concern for leaders.

Increase in unit labour cost **alone does not necessarily mean a lack of competitiveness**, it can be attributed to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>increase in wages but a decrease in productivity performance</span>.

**Increase in wages** is important in a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>advancing economy</span> but this means that productivity will also need to increase. Companies  who can give workers wage increases and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>still remain profitable & competitive</span>.

Thus Singapore or companies will need to:
- Find ways to strengthen productivity
- Keep costs in check
- All without compromising on gains in income

Continuous innovation is the key to solve this. In the past innovation is not as important as productivity because Singapore was a developing country and is very far from the production possibility frontier

Making the leap to innovation is the hardest part. And <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>failure is important</span>.

> [!info] Failing forwards
> It is essentially to fail safely, smartly and to move on quickly

> [!note] Creative process
> Before you can **allow a product or service to be used in the global markers**:
> 
> - Need resources and man hours to come up with patterns
> - Take this patten into production, testing which market segment to sell this to
> - Financing & infrastructure needed
> - Mentoring
> - Synergy with other sectors 

Expenditure was poured into R&D (*research and development*) which manpower costs consistently accounted for 40 to 45% from 2007 to 2008

> [!question] How did Singapore ensure performance?
> - Leverage on multinationals
> - Exports
> - Global production network

Singapore is a **hub for multinationals**, which give the assurance to a foreign investors that their <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>investment will be safe</span>. It also has a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>good geographical location where no disasters happen</span>.

MNC's to be successful should globalise their manufacturing.

## Comparative Taxes

Singapore government spending is 12.6% of GDP while government revenue is 17.8% GDP which is higher than in most countries. However this **does not necessary means it its good or bad**.

> [!question] How does Singapore pay for its expenditure?
> <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>Taxes are one way to raise capital</span> for the government and one popular way is known as **GST**.

Taxes are a **financial tradeoff** as <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>taxes cost more than the amount raised</span>. This trade off is to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>discourage productive behavior</span>.

> [!info] Deadweight losses
> They represent <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>real economic costs of taxation</span>.

GST is a **consumption tax** which <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>taxes people for any amount spent</span>. Small companies do not need to collect GST but when purchasing materials they need to pay GST and thus will increase prices.

> [!question] Why have other taxes when Singapore has GST?
> There are other taxes like income and corporate taxes. This is because the deadweight losses of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>taxes rises as tax burden gets heavier</span>. 
> 
> It is <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>better to spread it out on multiple taxes</span>

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>Consumption taxes are regressive</span>, meaning it <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>gives more burden on people with lower income</span> and thus consumption tax is taxing the lower income households higher (*not because they pay more but relative to their income*).

> [!note] How can we help the poor
> **Tax exemption**:
> > Consumption taxes should be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>exempted from necessities</span> however, this <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>does help the poor but also gives a tax break for the rich</span>
> 
> **Transfer policies**
> > Why not <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>give back some money taken from these taxes</span>. This is known as a transfer where the government gives back money from taxes, like the Singapore GST voucher schemes. <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>This method is more fair</span> as richer households will not get back as much as the poor.
## Wages & Income Transfers

To **reduce income inequality** we can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>distribute the income evenly</span>. In the **long run** the government can achieve this by <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ensuring everyone has equal access to education & training</span> to raise their skills.

> [!question] Why some jobs pay less?
> One main reason is **supply & demand**. With high supply, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>value of the job's productivity is low</span>

If income **support policies are too generous**, it will be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>unsustainable</span> and low income people will be in a long run <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>disadvantage</span>.

What about in the **short run**, how can the government help?
### Minimum Wages

The government can enforce a minimum wage law which <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>requires employers to pay a minimum amount</span>. This is paid by employers, employees and consumers.

> [!failure] Trade off
> If the minimum wage policy is successful, it can lead to <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>unemployment</span></b>.
> 
> This is because many people will want to apply for this job, but employers will not want to hire them because it is not economical.

The government can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>set different minimum wages</span> for different **jobs** or different **groups of people**.
### Negative Income Tax

Instead of taking money from the poor, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>government can supplement</span> it. An example of this is the workfare income supplement (WIS). This is paid by taxpayers.

Essentially, the low income people will <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>not have their income taxed but rather supplemented</span>.

> [!important] The rate of declining supplement matters
> **Decrease too much** and the people <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>will not want to upskill</span>. This is because by increasing their income by a little will make them <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>lose out on a lot of supplements</span> .
> 
> **Decrease too little** and the tax will be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>helping those who do not need it</span>.

## Aging Life Cycle

As a **child or an elderly**, they <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>consume more than they produce</span>. But as an **adult** they <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>produce more</span>. Thus there <b><span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>needs to be a balance</span></b>.

> [!info] Pension system
> A scheme to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>reduce the need for the elderly to rely on working adults</span>.

### Pay As You Go Pension Scheme

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>current working adults, will support the current generation of elderly</span>, through taxes. An example of this is in the US.

> [!warning] Balance between working and retired people
> If there are **less working adults** and those who retire then there will be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>more burden</span> on the working adults.

### Funded or Defined Contribution Scheme

It is where <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>working adults invest a bit for retirement</span> and when they become elderly, the amount invested will be returned. An example of this is the CPF system in Singapore.

Elderly in Singapore also gets <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>subseries from healthcare and other services</span> all paid for by working adults.
## Retirement Adequacy

In 2014 half of the active CPF members turning 55 have enough to meet the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>retirement sum</span> (*$155,000*).

> [!info] Retirement sum
> It is the amount of money needed at retirement to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>provide for a basic standard of living during retirement years</span>.

But this retirement sum will be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>given as a annuity</span> of around $1200 to $1300 monthly. 

But over the years, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>retirement sum has been increasing</span>, mainly due to
- Increase in cost of living
- Life expectancy has been increasing

> [!note] What determines retirement wealth
> 1) **Contribution rate**
> 2) **Interest rate of return**
>    
> In summary the<span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'> more you invest</span> and the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>higher the interest</span> rate the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>more retirement funds</span> you have.

CPF is a **safe investment**, which means the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>returns will not be as high</span>.

Why the **CPF contribution rate changes**:
- It affects wage cost
- It can manage the economy
- CPF has changed from a simple retirement saving schemes to a more comprehensive social investment scheme (*can be used for healthcare, housing and not just retirement*)

> [!example] How well has CPF done
> $1 in 1970 is now $3.13 in 2010 in terms of purchasing power. And our CPF interest rates will double the amount if we invest $1 in 1970 till 2010

However if we **compare it to a pay as you go pension system**, the return from a Pay as you go system is approximately the same as the rate of economic growth. If we were to compare the returns from CPF to Singapore's economic growth it will <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>look modest</span>.

> [!note] Pay as you go pension schemes in Singapore
> - Pioneer generation package
> - Silver support schemes

So should Singapore move to a pay as you go pension system. No as it will <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>not be sustainable</span>:
- **Economic growth is likely to be slower** in the coming years
- **Singapore's aging society**
## Private Transport Policy

Private transportation in Singapore is expensive, but why is there this <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>price difference</span>:
- Open market value
- GST & excise duty
- Additional registration fee
- Certificate of entitlement (*COE*)

> [!info] Certificate of entitlement
> The COE prices are determined by an auction by the land transport authority. This is essentially the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>right to buy a car</span> in Singapore.

However there is a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>hidden cost</span> if Singapore **lets car ownership be unrestricted
- **Congestion** (*but we do not pay the full cost*)
- **Emissions**

> [!example] How other countries are handling congestion
> **Beijing**
> > Lottery system, where if you do not own a car you can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>sign up for free</span>
> 
> **Mexico**
> > Car plates with odd numbers can drive on 1 day and even numbers on another, essentially cutting the amount by half (*rich people can buy more cars to drive everyday*)

Singapore is also doing <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>road pricing</span>, to control congestion. But all this payment systems has its own issues such as income inequality.
