# Liabilities
---
In short a <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>liability</span> is an expense that has not been fully paid yet. These are <span style='color:#f7b731'>recognised as current liabilities</span> and they are reported on the balance sheet.

There is also <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>estimated liabilities</span> which may or may not happen in the future whose amount is estimated, some examples are warranty claims or lawsuits.

## Current Liabilities

### Payroll

Anything related to payroll (**Employee Salary**) are considered as <span style='color:#f7b731'>operating expenses</span>. And only <span style='color:#f7b731'>if it is not paid</span> it will then be <span style='color:#f7b731'>considered as a liability</span>.

Some **payroll related expenses** are :
- Employee CPF (Singapore, 17% employer , 20% employee)
- Personal income taxes
- Life insurances
- Payment required to agencies

The <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>net pay</span> is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>final amount</span> that the employee will receive <span style='color:#f7b731'>after all deductions</span> have been maid to their <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>gross pay</span>. These deductions are <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>recorded under payables</mark></b>.

<span style='color:#fa8231'>Before</span> a company pays employee salaries :
- **Debit** salary expense, employer CPF expense,  (<b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>CPF contributed by the company not the employer</mark></b>), income tax expense.
- **Credit** Various payables like salary payable, CPF payable

<span style='color:#fa8231'>When</span> a company pays employee salaries :
- **Debit** various payables
- **Credit** Cash

If <span style='color:#fa8231'>salaries are delayed</span> for a long period of time, <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>fines can be imposed</span>.
### Taxes

#### Sales Tax
In most places when a company makes a sale there will usually be <span style='color:#f7b731'>tax on that sale</span> by the government (GST/VAT). This is known as <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>sales tax</span>.

Take note that some times companies price their products
- **Including taxes**
- **Not including taxes** (Need to add on the tax as the final product cost)

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>makes a purchase</span> :
- **Debit** inventory or whatever asset being purchased (<b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Amount is before tax</mark></b>)
- **Debit** input tax (This account is for <span style='color:#f7b731'>purchases</span>)
- **Credit** cash if it is bring paid else under accounts payable (Amount is <span style='color:#f7b731'>with tax</span>)

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>makes a sale </span>:
- **Debit** cash where the <span style='color:#f7b731'>sale includes the tax amount</span>
- **Credit** sales revenue (Excluding tax amount)
- **Credit** output tax (This account is for <span style='color:#f7b731'>sales</span>)

To <span style='color:#fa8231'>calculate tax payable</span> :
$$
\text{Tax payable} = \text{Output Tax} - \text{Input Tax}
$$

Thus when <span style='color:#fa8231'>recording tax payable</span> :
- **Debit** output tax
- **Credit** input tax and tax payable

#### Property Tax
There is also <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>property tax</span> which are imposed on buildings, land or on other assets imposed by the government.

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>prepays property tax</span> :
1) **During prepayment**
	- **Debit** prepaid property tax
	- **Credit** cash
2) **When the tax is supposed to be paid**
	- **Debit** property tax expense
	- **Credit** prepaid property tax

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>does not prepay property tax</span> :
- **Debit** property tax expense
- **Credit** cash
## Provisions & Contingent Liabilities

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Provisions</span>
>It will be recognised when <span style='color:#f7b731'>losses are probable</span> and a <span style='color:#f7b731'>reliable estimate</span> can be made of the amount of the obligation

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Contingent Liabilities</span>
>Are <span style='color:#f7b731'>disclosed</span> in notes to financial statements if certain conditions are met

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Provisions</span> are <span style='color:#f7b731'>reported as an estimate liability in the balance sheet</span> while <span style='color:#f7b731'>contingent liabilites are not reported</span> in the balance sheet.

**When to recognise**

|           Probability            | Reliability of Estimate |                                                 Action                                                  |
| :------------------------------: | :---------------------: | :-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------: |
|     Very likely ($\gt 50\%$)     |           Yes           | Estimate the obligation and <span style='color:#f7b731'>recognise</span> as a liability (**Provision**) |
|     Very likely ($\gt 50\%$)     |           No            |                  Disclose in the financial statement notes (**Contingent Liability**)                   |
| Possible ($10\% \le x \le 50\%$) |           Yes           |                  Disclose in the financial statement notes (**Contingent Liability**)                   |
| Possible ($10\% \le x \le 50\%$) |           No            |                  Disclose in the financial statement notes (**Contingent Liability**)                   |
|       Remote ($\lt 10\%$)        |           NA            |                                               Do nothing                                                |
If it is <span style='color:#fa8231'>recorded as a liability</span>:
- **Debit** the account other damages/lawsuit loss
- **Credit** the account other provision/lawsuit provision

This does not only apply to lawsuits but also <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>warranty</span> as well. For warranty, the estimated expense and the provision is <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>done at the time of sales</mark></b>, unlike lawsuit.

When a company <span style='color:#fa8231'>records warranty</span> :
- **Debit** product warranty expense
- **Credit** product warranty provision

When someone <span style='color:#fa8231'>claims warranty</span>
- **Debit** product warranty provisions (<span style='color:#f7b731'>No effect on warranty expense</span>)
- **Credit** supplies
- **Credit** wages payable / cash (**If labour was used**)

For <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>other revenue and expense</span> are items that are <span style='color:#f7b731'>incurred outside of the companies normal operations</span>, these include :
- Investments
- Losses from investments
- Costs from investing

This <span style='color:#fa8231'>segregation</span> is to to help investors <span style='color:#f7b731'>differentiate income</span> earned from operating activities from income earned <span style='color:#f7b731'>from peripheral activities</span>.

To <span style='color:#fa8231'>calculate earnings per chare</span> :
$$
\frac{\text{Net income for the year}}{\text{Average number of shares outstanding}}
$$
To <span style='color:#fa8231'>calculate average number of shares outstanding</span> :
$$
\frac{\text{Number of outstanding shares at the start of the year} + \text{End of the year}}{2}
$$
