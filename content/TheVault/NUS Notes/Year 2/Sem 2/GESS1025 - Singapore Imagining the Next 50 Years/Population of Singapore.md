# Singapore & Population
---
In Singapore or any country, there are some common <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>factors which affects the population</span> of a country:
- **Fertility**
- **Morbidity**
- **Mortality**
- **Migration**

The **main issue with Singapore's population** is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>aging population or the declining birth rate</span>. This is due to the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>following factors</span>:
- **Late marriages**
- **Late child birth**
- **Low fertility**

> [!danger] Implications for Singapore & its birthrate
> With an again population, it will result in a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>lower level economic productivity</span> & <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>decreased economic growth</span>
## Population Around the World

If we were to **analyse the population trends around the world** it will shed some light on the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>population issues in Singapore</span>.

> [!summary] History of the world's population
> In the early years, the **population growth was slow** even with **high birth rates** because of <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>high death rates</span> but as time went on, mortality rates decrease causing a rapid increase in population.

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>rise in population has caused problems</span> for many countries in terms of:
- **Environment** (*From industrialism*)
- **Declining working population**

> [!info] Dependency Ratio
> It is the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ratio of working population against those who cannot work</span> (*children & elderly*).
> 
> This problem, assumes that <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>migrants can fill in the gaps</span>.
## Population of Singapore

During the **British colonial days**, Singapore's population was <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>increasing at a steady rate</span>. 

However with the changes in migration (*China & India*) patterns made Singapore experience lots of immigrants. And when **Singapore industrialise**, they need so <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>supplement the workforce with migrants</span>.

This resulted in rising migrants and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>decreasing Singapore citizens</span>. Coupled with the fact that with **lower fertility and lower mortality rates**, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>average age distribution has shifted upwards</span>.
# Low Birth Rate
---
For a **small nation state** like Singapore, the **low birth rate** is a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>national crisis</span>. This is because of the country need to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>rely on the people</span> with no natural resources.

> [!info] Some terms about birth rates
> - **Crude birth rate** : Number of live births per year for every 1000 people
> - **Total fertility rate** : Number of children born to a woman till the end of their childbearing years
> - **Replacement level fertility** : Average number of children to replace the population (*Usually above 2*)

Singapore's total fertility rate (*1.25*) is lower than the replacement level fertility, it has <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>never gone close to 2</span>.

> [!abstract] City Centers vs Rural Areas
> Globally, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>city centers suffer more than rural areas</span> when it comes to **low birth rates**.
> 
> And Singapore, is not a rural state.

While the usual factors plays a part for lower birth rates, the **importance of higher education did affect birth rates**. Since it is welcomed by many industries, this means <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>people pursue higher education</span> which means <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>later entry to the workforce</span> and then <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>later family formation</span>.

> [!question] Why study on fertility rate?
> There are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>many aspects</span> associated with fertility rate:
> - **Support growth and maintenance** of the potential labour force (*Can replace the working population*)
> - Provides the **foundation for military capability**
> - **Sustain consumption**, the needs of young and their consumption is different from old people.
## Why is there Low Birth Rate in Singapore

<span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Aspirations of young Singaporeans</span> differs and might not feel the need to start a family. There is also an <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>element of choice</span>. But is this choice <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>conditioned by social environment</span>?

> [!question] Why the declining birth rate with a pro family government?
> Singapore's government **supports family creation and has multiple incentives** for marriage and procreation (*BTO, subsidies for assisted reproductive technologies*). But because of these <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>social forces, it drives the want to procreate</span>.

In Singapore, the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>importance of career has give an advancement to women</span>. Women has the same opportunities as men and thus might want to put an importance in career development.

Another factor is a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>work life balance</span>. Some might need to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>work more to be financially stable</span> (*less time to take care of their child*). It is also quite <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>taxing to put in time and effort </span>to care of more children.

Singapore as a developed country and is showcased to have a good quality of life, made <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>people aspire to have a better lifestyle</span> (*working more*).

> [!note] The importance of paid work
> The return of investment empowers people to work harder. This results in <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>more economic power</span>, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>social power</span> (*social status*) and <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>negotiation power</span>.
> 
> Thus we need to <b><mark style='background:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>ensure that the returns of family formation matches paid work</mark></b>. Currently the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>returns of a family will take very long</span> to notice (*parents not being alone and bring taken care of*).

The <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>advances of healthcare</span> also affected the rate of birth. Since many successful attempts shows that people are giving birth at older ages giving the feeling that <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>healthcare has surpass blockage of natural birth</span>.
# Aging Society
---
The WHO deemed that an <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>aged society</span> is where **14% or more of the population is aged 65 or above**. Which Singapore is projected to become in the coming years.

With a [[Population of Singapore#Low Birth Rate|low fertility rate]], coupled with <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>high life expectancy</span> of Singaporeans (*82.5 years in 2013*) were some of the factors for Singapore's aging society.

> [!check] High life expectancy
> This does not mean that it is a bad thing, as it reflects on a countries, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>better healthcare</span>, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>quality of life</span>, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>declining childhood mortality</span>.
## Problems Arising from Aging Population

With a aging population, the dependency ratio will be standing on its point, meaning there will be <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>less manpower</span>.

This will affect the economy of Singapore as <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>companies will need to supplement the missing manpower</span> and in the worst case might cause more <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>economic burden on the working-age population</span>.

The government will also <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>invest more on healthcare</span> to take care of the elderly (*Make it more accessible & affordable*).
## Address Concerns about Aging Population

In the early days **Singapore has its own aging policies**, which <span style='color:var(--mk-color-orange)'>focuses on</span>:
- **Harmonious relations** between generations
- **Dignity** & **affordable life** to aged population

But now aging policies should <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>address the current generation's characteristics and demands</span> because they will be making up the elderly in the future.

In terms of economy, Singapore should **not disregard old people as not being able to work**, they have <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>more experience & knowledge</span>. This means a **higher emphasis in education**.
# Migration
---
<span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>Migration</span>, is the **movement of people in & out of a specified sovereign territory**.

$$
\text{Net migration} = \text{In-migration rate} - \text{Out-migration rate}
$$
During British occupation Singapore's population was largely due to immigration. But now with **industrialisation and lack of manpower** (*low birth rate*), immigrants are <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>hired for Singapore's economic growth</span>.

Now the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>large part of Singapore population are immigrants</span> and not Singapore citizens.

> [!question] Increase PR & immigrants a bad thing?
> Not necessary all bad, there are some positives such as <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>increasing social and cultural diversity</span> and as mentioned before, <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>economic growth</span>.

**Singapore's immigration and population policies** has <span style='color:var(--mk-color-red)'>received backlash</span> from citizens:
- Caused more competitiveness in jobs
- Overcrowding
- Tight housing market
- Loss of national identity

This caused Singapore to <span style='color:var(--mk-color-green)'>stricken its immigration policies in the workforce</span>.
## Challenges & Concerns in Singapore about Migration

With migration, it bring about different types of people and thus there is a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>mix of culture</span> and how citizens can <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>live together with one another in a small space</span>.

It also affects the <span style='color:var(--mk-color-turquoise)'>economy</span> since Singapore does <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>hire foreign talent & workers</span> (*Foreign aspirations*).

With migrants, Singapore faces a challenge of achieving
- **Economic pragmatism**
- **Identity of a nation**
- **Produce liveable urban environment**

There is also a <span style='color:var(--mk-color-yellow)'>shift in livelihood</span>, for Singaporeans, for instance, families reaching to caretakers which are mostly immigrants.
