# Inventory
---
In every transaction, there will be an increase in <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>cost of goods sold</span> (**COGS**) or <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>cost of sales </span>(**COS**) and a decrease in inventory.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Inventory</span> - Current asset in the balance sheet, which is <span style='color:#f7b731'>purchases to be resold to customers</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>COGS</span> - Is an operating expense in the income statement

**Revenue - COGS = Gross profit** on the income statement.

The <span style='color:#fa8231'>value of inventory comprises of all of these cost</span> :
- Cost of buying the goods
- Cost of transportation
- Other taxes
- Handling Costs

Basically anything directly attributed to the acquisition of goods <span style='color:#f7b731'>except sales and marketing</span>.

**Ownership**
>When <span style='color:#fa8231'>goods in transit</span>, sometimes the <span style='color:#f7b731'>ownership of the goods is passed</span>

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Free-on-board destination</span> - Ownership of the goods is passed when the <span style='color:#f7b731'>buyer receives it</span>
<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Free-on-board shipping point</span> - Ownership of the goods is passed <span style='color:#fa8231'>once goods are in transit</span>

Sometimes <span style='color:#fa8231'>inventory</span> can be <span style='color:#fa8231'>held on behalf</span> of a third party (**Consignee - Consignor Relationship**). Ownership does not belong to the third party player (**Consignee**). For example Best Denki.
## Methods of Recording Inventory

There are 2 ways of doing this :
1) **Perpetual**
2) **Periodic**
### Perpetual Method
Every purchase and transaction is recorded on a <span style='color:#f7b731'>continual basis</span>. Detail records of the number of units on hand and the cost of each purchase and sales are preprepared throughout the year.

<span style='color:#f7b731'>Records are digitized</span> and companies who use this methods have <span style='color:#f7b731'>high value inventory</span> items or when there are large costs to understocking and overstocking.

Everything will be <span style='color:#f7b731'>under inventory</span>, unlike the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>periodic method</span>.

Tracking is done as and when, and therefore,
- Ending inventory will be in the balance sheet
- Cost of goods sold will be in SCI
### Periodic Method
Instead of recording it daily, it will only be <span style='color:#f7b731'>recorded at the end of the year based on physical stock take</span>.

There is <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>no detailed records</span> of what goes in and out, no records of the amount of COGS and inventory throughout the year.

To combat this, there are <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>temporary accounts</span> which will be converted at the end of the accounting year.
Such accounts terms are
- **Purchases** for any buying of inventory, debit to increase
- **Freight In** for any transportation cost, debit to increase
- **Purchase Returns** for any returning of inventory, credit to increase
- **Purchase Discounts** for any discounts received, credit to increase
- **Sales** for any inventory sold, note that <span style='color:#f7b731'>COGS are not applicable</span>, credit to increase

When <span style='color:#fa8231'>reporting at the end of the year</span>
- Adjust based on temporary account balances by reversing the above. In the end inventory will be debited by the total ($\text{Purchase } + \text{Freight in } - \text{Purchase Returns } - \text{Purchase Discounts}$).
- Do a stock take at then end of the year, <span style='color:#f7b731'>calculate quantity and inventory costing</span> to determine <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>ending inventory cost</span>
- Compute **COGS** which is $\text{Opening inventory balance} + \text{Net Purchase} - \text{Ending inventory balance}$
- Then with the **COGS** amount, <span style='color:#f7b731'>credit COGS and debit Inventory</span>.

**Shrinkage**
>It happens when the physical <span style='color:#f7b731'>count is different from</span> the one inside the <span style='color:#f7b731'>book</span>

**Entry :**
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit cost of goods sold</mark></b> and then <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit inventory</mark></b>

# Inventory Costing
---
Under the <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>periodic system</span>, there are many ways to calculate the cost of inventory and **COGS**.

1) **Specific Identification**
>For every item, <span style='color:#f7b731'>record its actual cost</span> which will be used for tracking and calculating

This is seldom used as it <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>takes up a lot of time and resources</span>, but can be used for high value inventory.

2) **FIFO** (First in first out)
>It is the assumption that the <span style='color:#f7b731'>first goods purchased will be the first good sold</span>

So for each sale, the <span style='color:#f7b731'>item cost for the oldest purchased goods </span>will be deducted off first.

3) **LIFO** (Last in first out)
>It is the assumption that the <span style='color:#f7b731'>last goods purchased will be the first good sold</span>

This is <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>not allowed under the IFRS</span> but is used in GAAP.

4) **Weighted Average Cost**
>It uses the <span style='color:#f7b731'>weighted average cost</span> of all the goods to calculate the ending inventory

To calculate the inventory it is just, the sum of its total inventory divide by the number of units.

**Comparisons**
- In times of <span style='color:#fa8231'>rising inventory prices</span> (the most common situation in most industries today), cost of goods sold is <span style='color:#f7b731'>highest with LIFO and lowest with FIFO</span>. Gross margin, net income, and ending inventory are lowest with LIFO and highest with FIFO. If management bonus is linked to net income, companies tend to prefer FIFO.
- The advantage of <span style='color:#20bf6b'>LIFO is the attractiveness of lower income tax</span>.
- FIFO measures inventory value better because it <span style='color:#f7b731'>approximates current cost</span>.
- WA and LIFO <span style='color:#eb3b5a'>understate ending inventory</span> when prices are increasing.
# Inventory Valuation
---
Some inventories may be <span style='color:#f7b731'>damaged</span>, become wholly or partially obsolete, or their selling prices may have declined.

In this case, the inventories should be <span style='color:#f7b731'>written down to their net realizable values</span>.

IFRS requires that company report on the balance sheet inventory value that is the lower of cost and net realizable value, to reflect realistic future economic benefits.

<span style='color:#0fb9b1'>Net realizable value</span> (NRV) is the <span style='color:#f7b731'>estimated selling price</span> in the ordinary course of business, less the estimated costs of selling, e.g., selling commission

If $NRV \lt Cost$, then carry out the following entry :
- <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>Debit cost of goods sold</mark></b> and then <b><mark style='background:#f7b731'>credit allowance for inventory write-down</mark></b>
- If $NRV \ge Cost$, nothing needs to be adjusted

The <span style='color:#0fb9b1'>allowance for inventory write-down</span> is a <span style='color:#f7b731'>contra-inventory account</span>, which <span style='color:#f7b731'>reduces the inventory cost</span> on the balance sheet.

Either adjust item by item which is usually done or, group similar or related items if appropriate.

**Inventory Turnover**
> A measure of the <span style='color:#f7b731'>efficiency</span> with which <span style='color:#f7b731'>inventory is managed</span>. <span style='color:#20bf6b'>The higher, the better</span>

$$
\text{Inventory Turnover } = \frac{\text{Cost of goods sold}}{\text{Average inventory}}
$$
**Number of Days Sales Inventory**
>Inventory turnover can be converted into the number of days’ sales in inventory. It <span style='color:#f7b731'>measures the average number of days from purchase to sales</span>. <span style='color:#20bf6b'>The shorter, the better</span>

$$
\text{Nunber of days sales in inventory } = \frac{\text{365}}{\text{Inventory turnover}}
$$
